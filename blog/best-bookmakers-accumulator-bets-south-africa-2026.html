What Is an Accumulator Bet?

An accumulator (also called a multi, parlay, or acca) combines multiple selections into a single bet. Every selection must win for the bet to pay out. The odds multiply together, which is why accas can turn a R50 stake into thousands — but it's also why they're harder to land than singles.

Here's a quick example with three PSL matches:

Match Selection Odds
Mamelodi Sundowns vs Sekhukhune United Sundowns to win 1.45
Kaizer Chiefs vs AmaZulu Chiefs to win 1.90
Orlando Pirates vs SuperSport United Pirates to win 1.65

Accumulator odds: 1.45 × 1.90 × 1.65 = 4.55

R50 stake returns: R50 × 4.55 = R227.25 (profit of R177.25)

Compare that to betting R50 on each match individually — you'd risk R150 total and your combined profit would be much lower even if all three win. That's the appeal of accas: concentrated risk, amplified reward.

But here's the catch: if Chiefs draw 1-1 and your other two legs win, you get nothing. That one slip kills the entire bet. This is exactly why features like acca insurance and cash-out matter so much — and why picking the right bookmaker is crucial.

How Accumulator Odds Work — Calculating Your Returns

Understanding how acca odds are calculated is essential before you start placing multi bets. It's simple maths, but it's worth nailing down.

The formula: Multiply all your decimal odds together, then multiply by your stake.

Let's say you're building a four-leg PSL acca for a Saturday afternoon:

  1. Sundowns to beat Cape Town City — 1.50
  2. Chiefs to beat Stellenbosch — 2.10
  3. Pirates to beat Richards Bay — 1.70
  4. Over 2.5 goals in Royal AM vs SuperSport — 1.85

Combined odds: 1.50 × 2.10 × 1.70 × 1.85 = 9.91

R100 stake: R100 × 9.91 = R991.00 potential return

That's nearly 10x your money from four selections. But remember — all four must win. If Chiefs draw, the entire acca loses.

5 Common Accumulator Mistakes (And How to Avoid Them)

Accumulators are exciting, but they're also where most SA punters lose the most money. Here are the five mistakes we see constantly — and how to avoid them. For a deeper dive, read our 5 common betting mistakes guide.

1. Too Many Legs

Every leg you add to an accumulator dramatically reduces your chance of winning. A three-leg acca with favourites might have a 30-40% chance of landing. A ten-leg acca? You're looking at under 1%, even if every selection looks "safe." The bookmaker's margin compounds with every leg — they love ten-fold accas because the house edge is enormous.

Fix: Keep your accas to 3-5 legs. The sweet spot for risk vs reward is a treble or four-fold. If you want longer accas, use Supabets' acca boost to offset the lower probability, or Hollywoodbets' insurance to protect your stake.

2. Combining Correlated Events Incorrectly

Putting "Sundowns to win" and "Under 1.5 goals" in the same acca seems logical if you expect a tight 1-0. But these selections are correlated — when Sundowns win, they often score 2 or 3. Bookmakers know this and adjust odds accordingly. You're not getting the value you think.

Fix: If you want correlated selections in the same match, use Betway's same game multi or Sportingbet's Build-A-Bet — these features calculate the correct combined odds for correlated events within a single match.

3. Ignoring the Draw

In the PSL, draws happen in roughly 25% of matches. Punters love backing favourites to win, but that "easy" Chiefs win at 1.70 has a draw lurking. When you stack five "easy wins" into an acca, the probability of at least one draw torpedoing your bet is surprisingly high.

Fix: Mix in over/under and both teams to score markets instead of just match results. A "Over 1.5 goals" selection at 1.25 is more likely to land than "Chiefs to win" at 1.70 — and it still adds to your combined odds.

4. Chasing Losses with Bigger Accas

Your Saturday treble lost because of a last-minute equaliser. So you build a seven-leg acca on Sunday to "make it back." This is how bankrolls evaporate. Longer accas have lower hit rates, and emotional betting leads to poor selection discipline.

Fix: Set a weekly acca budget and stick to it. Two or three well-researched trebles are better than one desperate ten-fold. Use our betting calculator to work out stakes that fit your bankroll.

5. Not Shopping for the Best Odds

A 0.10 difference in odds on one leg doesn't feel like much. But across a four-leg acca, those small differences compound. Getting 1.95 instead of 1.85 on one selection can add 10-15% to your total return.

Fix: Compare odds across bookmakers before placing. Check our PSL Match Centre for live odds comparisons. Having accounts with multiple bookmakers isn't just smart — it's essential for serious acca bettors.

Accumulator Betting FAQ

What's the minimum number of legs for an accumulator?

Two. A two-leg accumulator is called a double. Three legs is a treble. Four and above are called four-folds, five-folds, etc. Most acca insurance and boost promotions kick in at 3-5 legs minimum.

Can I cash out an accumulator early?

On Betway, Hollywoodbets, and Sportingbet — yes. If your first few legs have landed, you can lock in a profit before the remaining legs are settled. The cash-out value depends on the remaining legs' live odds. Gbets and Supabets have more limited cash-out options on accas.

What happens if a match in my acca is postponed?

Most SA bookmakers void the postponed leg and recalculate your acca without it. A four-fold becomes a treble. Your bet isn't lost — it just has one fewer leg and a lower combined return.

Are accumulator bets good value?

Honestly? The bookmaker's margin compounds with every leg, so the house edge on a ten-fold is much higher than on ten individual singles. Accas are fun and can return big payouts, but for consistent profit, serious bettors mix accas with singles and doubles. Don't make ten-fold accas your entire strategy.
