Golf Betting Markets Explained

If you're new to golf betting, here's a breakdown of the markets you'll find at SA bookmakers:

Outright Winner

The simplest bet: pick who wins the tournament. In golf, this is hard — fields are 144+ players and the favourite typically has a 10-15% implied probability. That's why odds of 8/1 or higher on the favourite are normal. This is a high-risk, high-reward market.

Each-Way Betting

The bread and butter of golf punting. An each-way bet is two bets: one on your player to win, and one on them to place (typically top 5 or top 8). If they win, both bets pay. If they place, only the place portion pays at a fraction of the win odds (usually 1/4 or 1/5).

Example: You back Bezuidenhout each-way at 20/1 with a R50 stake (R100 total — R50 win, R50 place). Betway pays 1/4 odds on top 8. If he wins, you get R1,000 (win) + R250 (place) + stakes back = R1,350. If he finishes 5th, you get R250 + R50 stake = R300. Use our each-way calculator to work out exact returns.

Top 5/10/20 Finish

Bet on a player to finish inside the top 5, 10, or 20. Lower odds than outright, but much higher probability of winning. A strong player at a home event might be 3/1 for a top 10 finish — that's a realistic punt.

Head-to-Head Matchups

The bookmaker picks two players and you bet on who finishes higher. This removes the "144-player field" problem and turns golf into a two-horse race. Great for punters who know player form but don't want to predict the outright winner.

First Round Leader / Round Betting

Bet on who leads after round 1, or which player shoots the lowest round on a specific day. These are fast-result markets that add excitement to each tournament day.

Nationality Betting

Top South African, top American, top English player — these are popular at SA events. For the SA Open, the "top South African" market lets you focus purely on the local battle.

Golf Betting Tips for SA Punters

1. Always Bet Each-Way on Longshots

Golf is one of the few sports where each-way betting consistently offers value. If you're backing a player at 25/1 or longer, always go each-way. A top 5 or top 8 finish at 1/4 odds still pays well, and it happens far more often than an outright win.

2. Check Course Form

Some golfers perform consistently well at specific courses. Before the SA Open, check how players have performed at Randpark in previous years. A player who's finished top 15 three times at the same venue is a better bet than a higher-ranked player with no course history.

3. Consider the Altitude

Joburg sits at 1,750m above sea level. The ball flies further in the thin air, which benefits long hitters. This is a genuine factor that bookmakers don't always price in. Dean Burmester's power game, for example, is amplified at altitude.

4. Line Shop Across Bookmakers

Golf odds vary more between bookmakers than most sports. A player might be 20/1 at Betway and 16/1 at Hollywoodbets. Always check our odds comparison page before placing a golf bet — the difference in each-way returns can be significant.

5. Don't Ignore the Cut

Around half the field gets cut after 36 holes. If your each-way selection misses the cut, you lose everything. Factor in consistency and cut-making ability, not just upside. A player who makes 80% of cuts is a better each-way bet than a boom-or-bust player who misses half.

Golf Betting vs Other Sports

If you normally bet on football or rugby, golf requires a different mindset:

  • Bigger fields = longer odds. Don't expect 2/1 favourites. The shortest-priced player in most golf tournaments is 6/1 to 10/1.
  • Each-way is king. In football, you bet to win. In golf, each-way betting is how experienced punters extract value from large fields.
  • Patience required. A golf tournament runs Thursday to Sunday. Your bet lives or dies over 72 holes, not 90 minutes.
  • Form is everything. Recent form (last 4-6 tournaments) matters more in golf than almost any other sport. Check the DP World Tour stats before betting.
