Odds Shopping and Accumulators

A lot of punters underestimate how much value disappears inside average prices. One bookmaker can look friendlier, louder or more familiar, but still quietly give you a worse long-term number. That problem gets worse on accumulators. If each leg is slightly weaker, the compounded result is much worse than it looks on the surface.

Say you build a three-leg weekend slip with a PSL favourite at `1.42`, a Springbok opponent line at `1.55`, and a cricket favourite at `1.48`. Combined, that is about `3.25`. If SuperSportBet improves one leg to `1.46` and Betway improves another to `1.58`, neither site is “the best” in isolation. The best slip might come from pricing each leg separately and deciding whether the convenience of one account is worth the lost return.

This is where the accumulator calculator earns its keep. Use it before adding a fourth short-priced leg just because the return suddenly looks lekker. Most weak accumulators are not ruined by one terrible pick. They are ruined by one unnecessary extra favourite.
