Carling Knockout Last 16 Betting Tips: Oct 27-28 2026

Published: 26 October 2026 • Category: PSL Betting • Reading time: ~8 minutes

CARLING KNOCKOUT CUP LAST 16

Tuesday 27 and Wednesday 28 October 2026 · Four ties · 19:00 kick-offs

The Carling Knockout Cup last 16 finishes this week. Four ties are left, and three of them land on one Tuesday night. Orlando Pirates, Mamelodi Sundowns and Kaizer Chiefs all play on 27 October. Durban City and Stellenbosch close the round on 28 October.

Cup ties are different from league games. There is no table to protect and no second chance. A mid-table side can beat a giant over 90 minutes, and a giant can scrape through on penalties. That changes which markets are worth your money. This guide covers the fixtures, the markets that suit one-off knockout football, and how to shop for the best price across South African bookmakers.

How to read each tie

Orlando Pirates vs Milford FC

Pirates start their defence of the trophy at home against Milford, a Motsepe Foundation Championship side. On paper this is the most lopsided tie of the round. The market will price Pirates very short to win in 90 minutes, so the straight win offers almost no value on its own.

The better question is how Pirates win. Heavy favourites in cup ties often rotate when the schedule is tight. A rotated side can still win, but it may not score three or four. Handicap lines and over/under goals markets are where the real decision sits. Wait for the team sheet before you commit to a big handicap.

Mamelodi Sundowns vs Marumo Gallants

Sundowns host at Loftus Versfeld. They are the strongest squad in the competition by depth, which matters in a midweek cup tie. Marumo Gallants have little to lose and will sit deep if they can. Low-scoring first halves are common in this kind of game. A first-half market or a "Sundowns to win either half" bet can be a cleaner angle than a big handicap.

Kaizer Chiefs vs TS Galaxy

Chiefs host TS Galaxy at FNB Stadium. This tie sits four days before the Soweto Derby, and Chiefs fans will be watching the squad rotation closely. If the coach rests key players, the price on Chiefs may be softer than the league form suggests. If he plays the strongest side, the market will already reflect it.

Our Soweto Derby betting preview covers the weekend match in full. Do not let the derby bleed into your Tuesday decision. Treat this as a separate bet with its own stake.

Durban City vs Stellenbosch FC

This is the only last 16 tie played on Wednesday, and the closest to an even contest on paper. Durban City host at Chatsworth Stadium. Stellenbosch are a league side with a settled style. Ties like this are where draw-no-bet and double chance earn their keep, because 90-minute draws and penalty shootouts are real outcomes.

Know what "to win" means before you bet

This is the most common cup betting mistake. A standard match result bet settles on 90 minutes plus stoppage time. If the tie goes to extra time and penalties, a bet on the home team to win still loses if the score was level after 90.

A "to qualify" or "to progress" market is different. It pays on whoever goes through, including extra time and penalties. The price is shorter, because it covers more outcomes. Check which market you are actually backing before you confirm the stake. Bookmakers word this differently, and the rules can vary by operator and by competition.

Quick rule: If you want the team that advances, back "to qualify". If you want the 90-minute result, use match result, draw no bet or double chance. Read the market rules on your bookmaker's page.

Markets that suit knockout football

  • To qualify: The cleanest market when you only care who goes through.
  • Draw no bet: Your stake comes back if the 90 minutes finish level. Useful in the closer Durban City tie.
  • Double chance: Covers two of three outcomes. Short odds, so keep stakes realistic.
  • Asian handicap: Lets you back a favourite without needing the straight win price. Check the line carefully, since half and quarter lines pay differently.
  • First-half result: Cup favourites sometimes start slowly. A first-half draw can be a solid pick.
  • Total goals: Under 2.5 suits tight ties. Over 2.5 suits mismatches where the big side goes for goal difference.

Build a Tuesday night accumulator the safe way

Three games kick off at the same time, so it is tempting to build a three-leg accumulator on all the favourites. Do the sums first. Each short-priced leg shaves a little off your edge, and one upset ends the bet. A cup upset is far more likely than a league upset.

Run your numbers in the accumulator calculator before you place anything. Compare the combined return to the same stake spread across single bets. Often the singles on the best-priced legs return more, with less risk of losing the lot to one 1-0 surprise.

A simple staking plan for cup week

  1. Set one budget for the whole round, not one per game.
  2. Pick the one or two ties where you have a real view. Skip the rest.
  3. Wait for team news. Squad rotation is the biggest swing factor in midweek cups.
  4. Compare the price at two bookmakers before you place the bet.
  5. Do not chase a loss on Tuesday with a bigger bet on Wednesday.

Cup football produces upsets every year. That is why bookmakers shorten the favourites and why flat staking works better than hunting for a big win.
