Investec Champions Cup Round 1 Betting Guide 2026: Bulls, Stormers, Lions and Sharks – SA Franchise Preview for October 16–18

Published: 12 October 2026 | By BetSorted Team | 14 min read

The wait is over. Why This Weekend Matters Beyond Round 1

Champions Cup pool rugby operates on a double-round-robin format across four rounds. This means the margin between a winning start and a losing start is enormous – a bonus-point win in Round 1 can set you up with meaningful table buffer before the European winter rounds in December and January. Every SA franchise knows this. The Bulls remember the 59–22 quarter-final humiliation at Northampton that stemmed directly from squad rotation and under-preparation in earlier European rounds. There is institutional pressure on every SA team to take this seriously from kick-off.

Bettors should also note that the Stormers are unbeaten in six URC matches this season and arrive in Champions Cup as the most in-form South African side in years. The Bulls have a mixed URC record (around mid-table) but their European home record at Loftus altitude is an entirely different story. The Lions beat Leinster 32–31 at altitude in URC Round 1 – which remains one of the most impressive results by any SA side in recent memory.

Bulls vs Stade Français – Loftus Does the Talking (2:00 PM SAST)

The Vodacom Bulls hosting Stade Français Paris is the most straightforward-looking result on the card, and the 1.45 home-win price reflects it. But "straightforward" in the context of Loftus Versfeld has a very specific meaning: the Bulls are strong favourites not because they are objectively dominant in every department, but because 1,300 metres of altitude, summer heat, and a fast Loftus surface make this venue genuinely nightmarish for European visitors.

Stade Français are a legitimate Top 14 side. They play high-tempo, skill-heavy rugby at the Stade Jean-Bouin and have produced results against some of the better European clubs. But their only previous visit to Loftus – a fixture in the previous Champions Cup campaign – became a 46–40 try fest that the Bulls edged in what was, frankly, a bonus-point demolition in all but the final scoreline. French sides tend to struggle with the altitude more than the English or Irish because their game plan relies heavily on sustained high-intensity defensive pressure, which degrades badly above 1,000m.

Handré Pollard back in a Bulls jersey has given them a game-management dimension that significantly reduces their variance. When Pollard is calm at 10, the Bulls stay structured even when the scoreboard tightens. That matters against a Parisian side who are capable of a purple patch that brings them back into games.

Best Bets: Bulls vs Stade Français

  • Bulls to win – 1.45: Valid anchor leg. The price is short but the altitude premium is real and not fully priced by European-focused bookmakers.
  • Bulls -8.5 handicap – approx. 1.88: The better value play. If you believe Loftus delivers a double-digit Bulls win (likely if Stade Français fade after 50 minutes at altitude), this is the entry point.
  • Both teams to score + Bulls win – approx. 1.75: Stade Français have enough attacking quality to cross at least once. This combination gives slightly better odds than the outright and keeps both teams honest.
  • Avoid: Stade Français to win at 3.00. Not impossible, but without a track record of winning at SA altitude it is a price chasing a very low-probability outcome.

Stormers vs Bristol Bears – Cape Town's Turn (4:15 PM SAST)

The DHL Stormers hosting Bristol Bears at DHL Stadium is the fixture where SA punters should feel the most confident about the home side. The Stormers are six wins from six in the URC – the best attacking and one of the tightest defensive records in the entire competition. They have not dropped a point in league rugby this season. Champions Cup Round 1 is a different beast, but the form heading in is extraordinary.

Bristol Bears are a solid Premiership side under Pat Lam. They play structured, physical rugby with a strong lineout and a reliable kicking game. In English conditions, against English or Irish opposition, they are competitive. In Cape Town in October, in summer heat, against a Stormers side in full flight with Cobus Reinach, Sacha Feinberg-Mngomezulu and Damian Willemse running the show? That is a very different ask.

DHL Stadium's wind and the Stormers' kick-and-chase game can be brutal for visiting sides who have not experienced Cape Town conditions. Bristol will be disciplined and hard to break down early. But if the Stormers build momentum through their set-piece and territory game, the pattern of the last several Stormers Champions Cup home performances suggests a comfortable margin is more likely than a tight finish.

Best Bets: Stormers vs Bristol Bears

  • Stormers to win – 1.50: Reasonable anchor price given the form and home conditions. Short, but defensible.
  • Stormers -7.5 handicap – approx. 1.88: The value vehicle if you believe the Stormers' dominant recent form holds at home. Worth the extra margin ask at this price.
  • Stormers to score 3+ tries – approx. 2.00: If your book offers this market, it is worth a look. The Stormers backline has multiple try-scoring threats and Bristol's defence, while organised, can be stretched in the wide channels.
  • Stormers half-time/full-time – approx. 1.60: The Stormers' home dominance tends to be built from the front. Leading at half time and maintaining it is their typical pattern.

La Rochelle vs Lions – The Hardest Draw in the Pool (6:30 PM SAST)

The 10bet Lions travelling to Stade Marcel-Deflandre in La Rochelle is, without a doubt, the most difficult SA fixture of Champions Cup Round 1. La Rochelle are serial Champions Cup finalists – they won the title in 2022 and 2023, were runners-up in 2024, and continue to field a squad that blends French physicality with a genuine ability to play smart, adaptable rugby regardless of conditions.

La Rochelle at home at approximately 1.65 as favourites is not a price born from arrogance. Their Stade Marcel-Deflandre has been a fortress in European rugby for years. The Atlantic coast conditions – low temperatures, potential wind and rain in October – are nothing like what the Lions train in at altitude in Johannesburg. The Lions are good enough to compete, but winning there in Round 1, in unfamiliar conditions, is a significant ask.

The Lions' qualification was sealed by their stunning 32–31 win over Leinster in URC Round 1, and their Pool 2 draw (alongside Toulouse, Saracens, La Rochelle, Exeter and Connacht) is probably the most competitive pool in the competition. To qualify from this pool, the Lions will need at least two wins and the Round 1 trip to La Rochelle is not the fixture to be banking on as a win. It is, however, a fixture where a competitive loss with a bonus point is a legitimate success metric.

Best Bets: La Rochelle vs Lions

  • La Rochelle to win – 1.65: The most straightforward call in this fixture. La Rochelle at home is a deserved favourite.
  • Lions +8.5 handicap – approx. 1.88: If you believe the Lions compete and keep it to single-digit deficit, the handicap is the play. The Lions have the quality to stay in this game even if La Rochelle win.
  • Lions to score a try anytime – approx. 1.80: Even away at La Rochelle, the Lions backline carries enough threat to cross at least once. Anytime try markets on SA franchises in European fixtures often carry value when the team is being underestimated in the market.
  • Avoid: Lions to win outright at 2.30. It is not impossible – they beat Leinster in Round 1 of URC – but backing a Road win in La Rochelle in Round 1 of Champions Cup is a low-conviction, high-risk play. Keep the stake tiny if you go there.

Sharks vs Lou Rugby – Challenge Cup Away (approx. 5:00 PM SAST)

The Sharks find themselves in the EPCR Challenge Cup this season – the second-tier European competition – after a difficult URC campaign that left them outside the Champions Cup qualification spots. Despite fielding a squad loaded with Springboks (Eben Etzebeth, Malcolm Marx, Makazole Mapimpi, Lukhanyo Am among others), consistency has proved elusive in the league.

The Challenge Cup is, paradoxically, an opportunity. As Challenge Cup holders – having beaten Gloucester 36–22 in the final at Tottenham – the Sharks know this competition well and know how to peak in knockout rugby. Lou Rugby (Lyon) are a Top 14 side and a credible opponent away from home, but this is not the Toulouse or La Rochelle scale of difficulty that awaits in a Champions Cup pool.

The Sharks at approximately 2.00 away is worth attention. That is an even-money-ish price for a team with Springbok quality throughout the squad, against a Lyon side that, while competitive in domestic French rugby, has not consistently punished SA opponents when given the chance. Eben Etzebeth's availability is a key variable here – any disciplinary suspension after recent red card proceedings would significantly affect the Sharks' set-piece and maul dominance.

Best Bets: Sharks vs Lou Rugby

  • Sharks to win away – approx. 2.00: The value play if Etzebeth and the first-choice Bok front row travel. Challenge Cup away rugby favours the side with superior individual quality, and the Sharks have that over Lou Rugby.
  • Sharks +3.5 handicap – approx. 1.75: Safer entry if you are not sure about selection. The Sharks keeping it within a converted try is a reasonable starting point.
  • Match total under 47.5 points – approx. 1.85: Away Challenge Cup games between French and South African opposition tend to be physical and forward-dominated, which usually keeps scores lower than open-play-optimised domestic fixtures. The under is worth a look.

The Bigger Picture: What SA Teams Need From Round 1

Champions Cup pool rugby rewards bonus points heavily. A team that wins with four or more tries gets five points rather than four. A team that loses by seven or fewer gets one bonus point. These small margins accumulate significantly over four pool rounds.

  • Bulls: Need a bonus-point home win to set themselves up for the December away rounds. Anything less is a wasted home advantage.
  • Stormers: Same logic. Six-from-six in URC momentum means a bonus-point home win is the minimum acceptable outcome. Anything less would be a genuine surprise.
  • Lions: A competitive loss with a losing bonus point in La Rochelle would be a solid Round 1 result. A win would be extraordinary. The home rounds in later rounds are where Lions need their points.
  • Sharks: A win in Lyon, whatever format, is the target. Being Challenge Cup holders means they carry psychological weight. A winning start sets up their European campaign properly.
