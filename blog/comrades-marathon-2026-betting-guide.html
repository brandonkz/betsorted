Comrades Marathon 2026 Betting Guide: Odds, Markets & Tips for the Down Run

Published May 15, 2026 | 10 min read

The Comrades Marathon is back on Sunday, June 8, 2026, and it's a Down Run year — meaning the race starts in Durban and finishes 87.7km later in Pietermaritzburg. For South African punters, Comrades is more than just an ultra-marathon. It's a betting event with unique markets, unpredictable conditions, and the chance to back local heroes chasing the podium.

This guide covers everything you need to know to bet smart on Comrades 2026: which bookmakers offer the best odds, what betting markets are available, top contenders for the men's and women's races, and strategies for finding value bets on South Africa's most iconic endurance race.

Comrades 2026 Quick Facts

  • Date: Sunday, June 8, 2026
  • Route: Down Run (Durban to Pietermaritzburg)
  • Distance: 87.7 km (54.5 miles)
  • Start time: 05:30 SAST
  • Cut-off time: 12 hours (17:30 SAST)
  • Expected field: ~20,000 runners
  • Prize money: R2.4 million total purse

The Down Run is historically faster than the Up Run (PMB to Durban), thanks to more downhill sections and a gentler finish. Expect winning times around 5h20-5h30 for men and 6h00-6h15 for women if conditions are good.

What Betting Markets Are Available for Comrades 2026?

1. Outright Winner (Men's / Women's)

The simplest bet: pick who you think will win the race. Odds typically range from 3.00 to 8.00 for favorites, with outsiders going as high as 50.00+.

Strategy: Don't just bet on the form favorite. Comrades is a mental and tactical race. Look at who's trained specifically for Comrades (not just international marathon stars), who's run the Down Run route before, and who has a proven record of finishing strong in hot conditions.

2. Top 3 / Podium Finish

Bet on a runner to finish in the top 3. Lower odds than outright winner bets, but safer if you like a runner but aren't confident they'll win.

Strategy: Good for backing a consistent performer who might not win but always medals. Look for runners with multiple Comrades medals who know how to pace the race.

3. Medal Finisher (Top 10)

A runner finishing in the top 10 earns a Gold Medal (under 6 hours for men, under 7:30 for women on Down Run). Some bookmakers offer "Top 10 finish" markets.

Strategy: Value bets on runners with strong training blocks but not household names. These markets can have softer odds because bookmakers focus pricing on the outright winner.

4. Head-to-Head Matchups

Pick which of two runners will finish higher. Common matchups include SA vs international runners, or two local favorites.

Strategy: Great for leveraging local knowledge. If you know one runner has struggled on Down Runs historically, bet against them even if their marathon times look similar.

5. Winning Time (Over/Under)

Some bookmakers offer total time markets, e.g., "Will the men's winner finish under 5h25?" or "Will the women's winner finish under 6h10?"

Strategy: Weather-dependent. If race day is forecast to be hot (25°C+), take the Over. If it's cool and overcast (15-18°C), take the Under. Down Runs are faster, but heat kills speed.

Betting Strategies for Comrades Marathon 2026

1. Check the Weather Forecast 48 Hours Before

Comrades races are won and lost on weather. A hot, humid day (25°C+) slows everyone down. Cool, overcast conditions (15-18°C) lead to fast times.

Hot day strategy: Bet on experienced runners who've raced Comrades in tough conditions (Mthembu, Molinaro). Avoid first-timers.

Cool day strategy: Back fast marathon runners who might struggle in heat but thrive in cool temps (Mothibi, Nyahora).

2. Value is in Top 3 / Top 10 Markets, Not Outright Winners

Outright winner odds on favorites are often too short to be worth it. Gerda Steyn at 2.20 means you risk R100 to win R220 — not great value given the unpredictability of ultra running.

Instead, look for Top 3 or Top 10 bets on consistent performers. Example: Carla Molinaro to finish Top 3 at 2.80 is better value than Steyn to win at 2.20.

3. Back Local Knowledge Over Marathon Times

A runner with a 2h08 marathon PB isn't guaranteed to dominate Comrades. Comrades requires:

  • Pacing discipline (don't blow up at 50km)
  • Tactical climbing (Polly Shortts is brutal if you hit it wrong)
  • Mental toughness (the cut-off pressure from 10 hours onward is intense)

Bet on runners who've finished multiple Comrades races, not just one-off marathon stars.

4. Head-to-Head Bets Are Gold If You Know the Runners

Bookmakers price head-to-heads based on recent marathon form, but Comrades is different. Use local intel:

  • Who trained on the actual Down Run route?
  • Who raced Two Oceans in April and might be fatigued?
  • Who has a history of dropping out vs. always finishing?

If you spot a mismatch (e.g., a marathon star vs. a seasoned Comrades racer), the head-to-head bet can offer serious value.

💡 Pro tip: Follow the runners on Instagram and Twitter in the weeks before Comrades. You can spot injury niggles, training struggles, or confidence levels that bookmakers haven't priced in yet.

What Makes the Down Run Different (and How It Affects Betting)

The Down Run (Durban to PMB) is faster but more technical than the Up Run. Key differences that affect betting:

  • Polly Shortts (km 45-50): Brutal uphill stretch that breaks runners who went out too fast. Bet on disciplined pacers.
  • Downhill finish: The last 10km are mostly downhill into PMB. Runners with good quad strength finish fast. Bet on runners with strong downhill running records.
  • Faster winning times: Expect winners around 5h20-5h30 (men) and 6h00-6h15 (women) if conditions are good. This affects Over/Under time markets.
  • Less attrition: Down Run has a higher finish rate (~85% vs ~80% on Up Run). Fewer DNFs = more competitive podium races.

Live Betting on Comrades: Is It Worth It?

Some bookmakers (Betway, Hollywoodbets) offer live in-play betting during Comrades, updating odds based on split times at key checkpoints (Drummond, Camperdown, Polly Shortts).

Live betting strategy:

  • Watch the live broadcast on SABC (or stream it)
  • If a favorite is struggling at the 50km mark (slow split at Polly Shortts), their odds will drift — time to back the chasing pack
  • If an outsider is sitting in the lead group at 70km, their odds will shorten — but they might blow up in the last 15km, so be careful

Live betting works best if you're watching the race and can react to real-time developments. Don't bet live if you're just guessing based on odds changes.

Common Comrades Betting Mistakes to Avoid

1. Betting on Marathon Form, Not Comrades Form

A runner who won the Cape Town Marathon in October might look great on paper, but Comrades is 87km, not 42km. Bet on proven ultra-marathon runners, not track stars making their Comrades debut.

2. Ignoring the Weather

A 5-degree temperature swing changes everything. Check the forecast 48 hours before and adjust your bets accordingly. Hot day = back experienced heat runners. Cool day = back speed merchants.

3. Overvaluing the Favorite

Gerda Steyn at 2.20 is not free money. Ultra running is unpredictable. Injuries, bad pacing, stomach issues — anything can derail a favorite. Look for value elsewhere unless you're very confident.

4. Betting Too Early

Odds shift right up until race day. If you bet on a runner in April and they pick up an injury in May, you're stuck. Wait until the week before Comrades when you have full injury reports and final entry lists.

Final Betting Tips for Comrades 2026

  • ✅ Bet on Top 3 / Top 10 markets for better value than outright winners
  • ✅ Back local SA runners who know the course and race tactics
  • ✅ Check weather forecasts 48 hours before and adjust your bets
  • ✅ Use head-to-head bets if you've researched the runners' Comrades history
  • ✅ Avoid betting on marathon stars making their Comrades debut — ultra running is a different beast
  • ✅ Watch for value on consistent medalists (Dijana, Molinaro) who always finish strong
  • ✅ Don't bet on favorites blindly — Steyn at 2.20 might be too short for the risk
⚠️ Remember: Comrades is the ultimate test of endurance and mental toughness. Even the best-laid bets can unravel in the last 20km. Bet responsibly, enjoy the race, and respect the runners who trained for months to reach that finish line.
