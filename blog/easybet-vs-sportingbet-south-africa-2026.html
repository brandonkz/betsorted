Betting Markets: Depth vs Simplicity

Sportingbet's Market Depth

Sportingbet offers significantly more markets per event. For a typical PSL match, you'll find:

  • Match result (1X2)
  • Double chance
  • Both teams to score
  • Over/under goals (multiple lines: 1.5, 2.5, 3.5)
  • Correct score
  • First goal scorer
  • Handicap betting
  • Half-time/full-time
  • Asian handicaps
  • Corner markets
  • Card markets

Easybet's Approach

Easybet covers the essentials — match result, over/under, both teams to score, and correct score for major events. It's more limited on prop markets and exotic bets. For most casual bettors, the core markets are all you need. But if you're the type who likes building same-game multis with cards, corners, and goal scorers, you'll hit the ceiling quickly on Easybet.

Mobile Experience

Both bookmakers work well on mobile, but the design philosophy is completely different.

Easybet Mobile

Clean. Fast. Minimal. You open it, find your sport, tap your bet, and you're done. The interface doesn't try to impress you with features — it just works. For punters who bet on their phone during a braai or at the stadium, this simplicity is a genuine advantage. Page loads are quick even on slower data connections, which matters in SA where not everyone is on fibre.

Sportingbet Mobile

More feature-rich but more complex. The app packs in more markets, live streaming, statistics, and in-play features. If you're comfortable navigating a busier interface, there's more to work with. But new users might feel overwhelmed compared to Easybet's clean layout.

💡 Our take: If you mainly bet on weekends using your phone, Easybet's simplicity wins. If you're doing in-play betting during matches and need live stats and deep markets, Sportingbet's app is the better tool. Read our full comparison of best betting apps in SA for more detail.

Deposits and Withdrawals

Deposits

Both bookmakers accept the standard SA payment methods — EFT, Ozow (Instant EFT), credit/debit cards, and voucher deposits. Minimum deposit is R10 on both platforms, which is about as low as it gets in SA.

Easybet has a slight edge for cash depositors thanks to 1Voucher support, which lets you buy vouchers at retailers like Pick n Pay and Shoprite. This is popular with bettors who prefer not to link bank accounts.

Withdrawals

Here's where Sportingbet pulls ahead. Their withdrawal processing is typically faster — most EFT withdrawals arrive within 24-48 hours for verified accounts. Easybet takes 24-72 hours on average, with some users reporting longer waits during busy periods (like after a big PSL weekend).

Welcome Bonuses

Sportingbet's welcome offer (up to R2,000 in free bets) is the larger of the two. Easybet offers up to R1,000. Both come with wagering requirements, so read the terms carefully before signing up purely for the bonus.

Here's the reality with welcome bonuses: they're nice, but they shouldn't be the deciding factor. The bookmaker you'll use for months or years matters more than a one-time bonus. Choose based on odds and markets, not the sign-up offer.

Lucky Numbers

Both Easybet and Sportingbet offer Lucky Numbers — the lotto-style betting product that's wildly popular in SA. The experience is similar on both platforms: pick your numbers, choose your draw, and wait for the results. Odds and payouts are comparable.

If Lucky Numbers is your main thing, there's no significant reason to choose one over the other. Both cover SA Lotto, PowerBall, UK49s, and international draws.

Horse Racing

Sportingbet has the clear advantage here. They cover all South African race meetings (Turffontein, Kenilworth, Greyville, Hollywoodpark, and more) plus international racing from the UK, Australia, Hong Kong, and Dubai. Their horse racing interface includes form guides, recent results, and jockey/trainer statistics.

Easybet covers SA racing but with less depth and fewer international options. If horse racing is important to you, Sportingbet is the better choice. See our full guide to horse racing betting in SA.
