What Is Esports Betting?

Esports betting is wagering on competitive video gaming. Professional teams and players compete in organised tournaments for games like Counter-Strike 2 (CS2), Dota 2, League of Legends (LoL), Valorant, and Call of Duty. These aren't backroom gaming sessions — we're talking packed arenas, prize pools in the millions of dollars, and global audiences bigger than many traditional sports events.

The 2025 League of Legends World Championship drew over 6 million concurrent viewers. The International (Dota 2's biggest tournament) regularly offers prize pools above $15 million. This is legitimate competitive sport, and the betting markets around it are maturing rapidly.

For South African punters, esports betting works exactly like traditional sports betting. You pick a match, choose a market (match winner, map winner, handicap, total rounds), check the odds, and place your bet. If you know how to bet on a Chiefs vs Pirates match, you already understand the mechanics.

Which Esports Games Can You Bet On in SA?

The five biggest esports for betting in South Africa are:

1. Counter-Strike 2 (CS2)

The king of esports betting. CS2 (the successor to CS:GO) is a tactical first-person shooter with a massive competitive scene. Major tournaments like ESL Pro League, IEM Katowice, and the CS2 Majors generate extensive betting markets. South Africa has a strong CS2 scene — teams like Bravado Gaming have competed internationally — so you may already be familiar with the game.

Common bet types: Match winner, map winner, map handicap, total rounds over/under, first to X rounds, pistol round winner.

2. Dota 2

A complex multiplayer online battle arena (MOBA) game with one of the biggest esports ecosystems. The International is the headline event, but the Dota Pro Circuit runs year-round with regional leagues and majors. Dota 2's betting markets are deep once you understand the game's dynamics.

Common bet types: Match winner, map winner, map handicap, first blood, total kills over/under, game duration over/under.

3. League of Legends (LoL)

The world's most-watched esport. LoL's competitive circuit includes regional leagues (LCK, LPL, LEC, LCS) and culminates in the World Championship. Betting markets are extensive during Worlds and major regional matches.

Common bet types: Match winner, map winner, first tower, first dragon, total kills, game duration.

4. Valorant

Riot Games' tactical shooter has quickly become one of the biggest esports. The VCT (Valorant Champions Tour) runs throughout the year with international tournaments. Growing rapidly in South Africa's gaming community.

Common bet types: Match winner, map winner, map handicap, total rounds, pistol round winner.

5. Call of Duty

The Call of Duty League (CDL) runs a franchise-based model similar to traditional sports. Less betting depth than CS2 or Dota 2, but available at major bookmakers during key events.

Common bet types: Match winner, map winner, map handicap.

💡 Pro tip: During major tournaments (Majors, Worlds, The International), even bookmakers that don't normally carry esports markets often add them. Check all your bookmaker accounts during these events.

How Esports Odds Work

If you've bet on football, rugby, or cricket in South Africa, esports odds work exactly the same way. SA bookmakers use decimal odds. Here's a quick refresher:

  • Favourite at 1.40: Bet R100, get R140 back (R40 profit)
  • Underdog at 3.50: Bet R100, get R350 back (R250 profit)
  • Even match at 1.90 / 1.90: Close to a coin flip — bookmaker takes a small margin

The key difference with esports is that odds can swing dramatically based on team news, roster changes, and map picks. A CS2 team might be 1.60 favourites overall, but on a specific map, they could be 2.50 underdogs. This is where esports knowledge gives you a genuine edge over the bookmakers — if you follow the competitive scene, you'll spot value that casual bettors miss.

Types of Esports Bets

Match Winner (Moneyline)

The simplest bet — who wins the match? In a best-of-3 (Bo3) CS2 series, you're betting on which team wins 2 maps first. Straightforward, easy to understand, and the bread-and-butter of esports betting.

Map Winner

Bet on the winner of a specific map within a series. Useful when you know a team is dominant on particular maps. For example, a team might be underdogs in the overall match but favourites on Map 1 if it's their best map. This is where deep game knowledge pays off.

Map Handicap

Similar to handicap betting in football. If you think Team A will win 2-0 in a Bo3, you can bet on Team A -1.5 maps at better odds than the straight match winner. If Team A does win 2-0, you win. If they win 2-1, you lose (because the handicap makes it 0.5-1).

Total Rounds/Maps Over/Under

Bet on whether the total number of rounds in a map (CS2/Valorant) or total maps in a series goes over or under a set line. Great for when you expect a close match (over) or a dominant performance (under).

Outright / Tournament Winner

Bet on who will win an entire tournament before it starts. Higher risk but potentially bigger payouts. Available on major events like the CS2 Major or Worlds.

Live / In-Play Betting

Bet during the match as it unfolds. Odds update in real time based on what's happening in the game. This is where esports betting gets really exciting — and really dangerous if you're not disciplined. Read our live betting guide for tips on staying sharp in-play.

Esports Betting Tips for SA Beginners

1. Start with one game

Don't try to bet across CS2, Dota 2, LoL, and Valorant simultaneously. Pick one game you enjoy watching and understand. Learn the teams, the map pool, the meta, the tournament format. Deep knowledge of one game beats surface knowledge of five.

2. Watch before you bet

Esports matches are freely available on Twitch and YouTube. Watch the teams you're considering betting on. Note how they perform on specific maps, how they handle pressure in close games, and whether they've made recent roster changes. This is free intelligence that most casual bettors ignore.

3. Understand map dynamics (CS2 & Valorant)

In CS2 and Valorant, the map veto process is crucial. A team might be dominant on Mirage but terrible on Anubis. Knowing team-specific map win rates gives you a massive edge when betting on individual maps or map handicaps.

4. Check for roster changes

Esports teams change rosters frequently. A team that was dominant last month might have lost their star player. Always check for recent roster changes before betting — a new player joining a team often takes weeks to integrate, and results can be inconsistent during that period.

5. Be cautious with live betting

Esports matches move fast. Momentum swings in CS2 can be dramatic — a team that's losing 3-12 can come back and win 16-14. Live betting is exciting but it's also where most beginners lose money. Start with pre-match bets and only move to live betting once you're comfortable reading how matches develop.

6. Manage your bankroll

The same bankroll management principles that apply to football betting apply to esports. Never bet more than 5% of your bankroll on a single match. Track your bets. Accept losing streaks as normal. Use our bankroll tracker to stay disciplined.

South Africa's Esports Scene

South Africa has a growing esports ecosystem, though it's still developing compared to Europe, Asia, or North America. Here's what's happening locally:

  • CS2: South Africa's strongest esport. Teams like Bravado Gaming and ATK Arena have represented SA at international events. The local competitive scene runs through platforms like ESEA and VS Gaming.
  • Dota 2: Smaller competitive scene in SA, but passionate. Bravado Gaming has fielded Dota 2 rosters in the past.
  • League of Legends: Growing community, supported by the ACGL (African Cyber Gaming League) tournament series.
  • Valorant: Rapidly growing in SA. The game's popularity among younger players is feeding into a competitive scene.
  • FIFA/EA FC: The biggest "casual esport" in SA, given football's popularity. VS Gaming has run major FIFA tournaments.

Betting markets on SA-specific tournaments are rare — the audience is too small for bookmakers to invest in pricing local matches. But the skills you develop following SA esports translate directly to betting on international events, where the markets are deep and the odds are competitive.

  • HLTV.org — The definitive CS2 statistics and news site. Team rankings, head-to-head records, map statistics.

FAQ

Is esports betting legal in South Africa?

Yes. Esports betting is legal in South Africa when offered by licensed bookmakers. The same gambling regulations that cover traditional sports betting apply to esports. All licensed SA bookmakers offering esports markets operate under provincial gambling board licences.

Which South African bookmakers offer esports betting?

Betway is the leading SA bookmaker for esports, with dedicated markets on CS2, Dota 2, League of Legends, Valorant, and more. 10bet and Sportingbet also offer esports markets. Most SA-focused bookmakers like Hollywoodbets and WSB have limited or no esports coverage.

What esports games can I bet on in South Africa?

The most commonly available esports for betting in SA are CS2 (Counter-Strike 2), Dota 2, League of Legends, Valorant, and Call of Duty. During major tournaments, some bookmakers also offer markets on FIFA/EA FC, Rocket League, and Rainbow Six Siege.

How do esports betting odds work?

Esports odds work exactly like traditional sports odds. In South Africa, bookmakers display decimal odds. A team at 1.50 means you'll receive R1.50 for every R1 bet (including your stake). A team at 3.00 is the underdog — R1 returns R3. Use our betting calculator to check exact returns before placing any bet.

Are there South African esports teams to bet on?

Yes. South Africa has a growing esports scene, particularly in CS2. Teams like Bravado Gaming and ATK Arena compete in international qualifiers and regional tournaments. However, betting markets on SA-specific tournaments are rare — most available esports betting covers international events like ESL Pro League, The International (Dota 2), and Worlds (LoL).
