Gauteng Gambling Board: What It Regulates, How to Check a Licence and Complain

Published 19 November 2026 · Sources checked 19 November 2026

If you place a bet in a shop in Soweto, play the tables at Montecasino or back a horse at Turffontein, the regulator in charge is the Gauteng Gambling Board (GGB). It licenses gambling venues and operators across the province, collects gambling taxes for the provincial government, and handles complaints from the public about its licensees.

This guide sticks to what the GGB and the National Gambling Board (NGB) publish themselves. We checked every contact detail, list and count below on 19 November 2026. Gauteng isn't the whole story for online betting, because many of the big online brands are licensed in another province, so we also explain how to tell which board your bookmaker answers to.

GGB at a glance
  • What it is: a statutory body set up under section 3 of the Gauteng Gambling Act, No 4 of 1995
  • Website: ggb.org.za (note: .org.za, not .co.za)
  • Address: 125 Corlett Drive, Johannesburg, 2018
  • Phone: (011) 581 4800 · Fax: (011) 581 4903
  • General email: info@ggb.org.za
  • Licensing: 011 581 4924 · licesing@ggb.org.za (spelled this way on the GGB site)
  • Race meetings: racemeetings@ggb.org.za · Totalisator: tote@ggb.org.za

What the Gauteng Gambling Board regulates

The GGB lists five kinds of gambling it regulates in the province:

  • Casino gaming
  • Betting on horse racing and sporting events
  • Bingo
  • Limited payout gaming machines, through route and site operators
  • The manufacturing and supply of gambling equipment

Betting is the oldest of these. The board notes that before August 1996, betting on horse racing and other sport was the only legal form of gambling in Gauteng. The Gauteng Gambling Act, promulgated that month, legalised the other forms and created the board.

What the board actually does

According to its published mandate, the GGB:

  • licenses people and companies to run gambling and related activities, and registers people who work in the industry
  • approves and registers gaming devices
  • collects gambling taxes and levies for the Gauteng government and other beneficiaries, and manages the Sports Development Fund
  • advises the provincial MEC on gambling matters
  • supervises the industry under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act, including FICA inspections, which is one reason your bookmaker asks for ID and proof of address (see our FICA verification guide)
  • receives and adjudicates complaints from the public about gambling disputes
  • works to stamp out illegal gambling

The GGB is also a Schedule 3C public entity under the Public Finance Management Act and a regulatory authority under the National Gambling Act, 7 of 2004.

Horse racing and the tote

The GGB's totalisator page lists 4Racing (Pty) Ltd, which it says is licensed to run horse racing and totalisator betting in seven of South Africa's nine provinces. 4Racing is based at Turffontein Racecourse, 14 Turf Club Street, Turffontein (+27 11 681 1500, customercare@4racing.com). Tote and race-meeting questions for the regulator go to tote@ggb.org.za and racemeetings@ggb.org.za. For more on betting on racing, see our horse racing betting guide.

How to check a Gauteng betting licence

  1. Find the licence details. The NGB says a legal operator should display its licence at its premises or on the home page of its website. Note the company name, licence number and province.
  2. Search the NGB register. Open the NGB verified operators list and search for the company name or licence number. The NGB asks punters not to gamble anywhere that isn't on that list.
  3. Check that the province matches. If the register says Gauteng, the GGB is your regulator. If it says Western Cape, KwaZulu-Natal or another province, that board is.
  4. For casinos, the GGB's casinos page lists the seven licensed venues above.

How to lodge a complaint with the Gauteng Gambling Board

The GGB's Legal & Law Enforcement unit investigates disputes and complaints from the public against licensees. The NGB sets out the order to follow:

  1. Complain to the operator first, in writing, and keep a copy.
  2. Go to the board that issued the licence. If it's the GGB, email info@ggb.org.za, call (011) 581 4800, or use the form on the GGB contact page.
  3. Escalate to the NGB at info@ngb.org.za, in writing, only if the provincial board hasn't been able to help. Our National Gambling Board guide explains what the NGB can and can't do.

Include your account or slip details, dates, amounts, any bet or transaction IDs, and screenshots or copies of your messages with the operator. A clear timeline makes it much easier for the board to act.

Reporting illegal gambling

The NGB's advice is to report illegal operators to your provincial board first. The board investigates and, if the operator is found to be illegal, refers it to the South African Police Service. In Gauteng that means the GGB, whose legal unit assists law enforcement in shutting down illegal gambling. Escalate to info@ngb.org.za if the board doesn't help.

Self-exclusion in Gauteng

Self-exclusion is a legal process that bars you from gambling. Here's how the GGB says it works:

  • How to apply: get a request form from the GGB or from any gambling operator's security department. The GGB publishes a self-exclusion form (PDF). Its home page says an online self-exclusion portal is launching soon.
  • How long it lasts: at least six months. It isn't automatically lifted after that. If you don't ask for it to be revoked, it can stay in force for life.
  • Support while excluded: you're offered counselling and support through the National Responsible Gambling Programme (NRGP). The GGB refers self-excluded punters to counselling through its partnership with the South African Responsible Gambling Foundation, and says the counselling costs you nothing. You can email helpline@sargf.org.za; the helpline number is at the end of this page.
  • Lifting it: you can only apply after six months, and you must show proof that you attended at least four counselling sessions with an NRGP treatment professional. The GGB then gives itself up to 30 days to revoke the order.
  • If you break it: it's your responsibility to stay away. You can be refused access and, in some cases, charged with trespassing.
  • Excluding someone else: to bar a partner or relative, the GGB says you need a court order.

The GGB's process is built around licensed gambling venues in Gauteng. If you bet online, also use the responsible gambling tools on each betting site you use, and see our responsible gambling page.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Gauteng Gambling Board?

The Gauteng Gambling Board (GGB) is the statutory body that regulates gambling in Gauteng, set up under section 3 of the Gauteng Gambling Act, No 4 of 1995. It licenses and oversees casinos, betting on horse racing and sport, bingo, limited payout machines and gambling equipment suppliers in the province.

What is the Gauteng Gambling Board's phone number?

The GGB switchboard is (011) 581 4800 and its general email is info@ggb.org.za. Its office is at 125 Corlett Drive, Johannesburg.

How do I check if a betting site or shop is licensed in Gauteng?

Find the licence number and company name on the operator's website or in the shop, then search for it on the National Gambling Board's verified operators register. If the province listed is Gauteng, the GGB is the regulator.

How do I lodge a complaint with the Gauteng Gambling Board?

Complain to the operator in writing first. If that fails and the operator is licensed in Gauteng, contact the GGB at info@ggb.org.za, on (011) 581 4800 or through the contact form on ggb.org.za. If the board cannot help, escalate in writing to the National Gambling Board at info@ngb.org.za.

How long does self-exclusion last in Gauteng?

A self-exclusion order lasts at least six months and is not lifted automatically. You can only apply to revoke it after six months, with proof that you attended at least four counselling sessions, and the GGB allows itself up to 30 days to process the request.

Are online betting sites licensed by the Gauteng Gambling Board?

Check each one. Many of the big online brands hold their licences in the Western Cape, even if they also run Gauteng betting shops. The NGB's verified operators register shows the province and licence number for each licence holder.
