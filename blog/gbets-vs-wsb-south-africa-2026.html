Odds & Margins — Who Gives Better Prices?

Neither Gbets nor WSB sits at the top of the SA odds table — that spot belongs to Betway and occasionally 10bet. But between these two, Gbets generally offers slightly tighter margins on international football and rugby, while WSB is more competitive on PSL and South African horse racing.

Here's a real-world example from a typical PSL match:

Market Gbets WSB
Chiefs to win 2.35 2.40
Draw 3.10 3.05
Pirates to win 2.80 2.85
Margin ~7.5% ~7.2%

The differences are small — we're talking a few cents per rand difference on most bets. If you're placing R50-R200 bets, you won't notice. If you're doing serious volume or building accumulators, even half a percent matters over time.

Sport Coverage — What Can You Bet On?

Gbets: The All-Rounder

Gbets covers 25+ sports including some you won't find at WSB. Their esports section is one of the best among SA bookmakers — CS2, Dota 2, League of Legends, and Valorant are all available with pre-match and live markets. They also have a decent tennis and golf offering with outright and tournament markets.

For SA sports, Gbets covers PSL, URC rugby, Currie Cup, cricket (Proteas + SA20), and horse racing. The market depth for PSL isn't as granular as WSB, but it's perfectly adequate for most bets.

WSB: The SA Sports Specialist

World Sports Betting earns its name when it comes to South African sport. Their PSL coverage is arguably the best of any SA bookmaker — you'll find player prop markets, corner totals, booking points, and half-time/full-time combinations that Gbets simply doesn't offer. Horse racing coverage is deep, with meetings from Turffontein, Kenilworth, Greyville, and Scottsville all listed with early morning prices.

Where WSB falls short is international depth. European football beyond the top 5 leagues is thin, and there's virtually no esports coverage. If you're into UFC, boxing, or niche sports, Gbets is the better choice.

🏉 Rugby fans: Both bookmakers cover Springboks and URC well. For the upcoming Nations Championship, you'll find similar markets at both. Gbets edges it for international rugby (Six Nations, etc.) while WSB is stronger on Currie Cup.

Gbets: R50 Free Bet + 50 Free Spins

Gbets gives you R50 in free bets plus 50 free spins on their casino when you register and verify (FICA). Mobile Apps & Usability

Gbets App

Gbets has a solid Android app (download the APK from their site — not on the Play Store due to Google's gambling policy). The interface is clean and modern, with smooth navigation between sports, live betting, and casino. Load times are decent, and the bet slip works without glitches.

They don't have an iOS app, but the mobile website is responsive and works well on Safari. Not ideal, but functional.

WSB App

WSB's app looks a bit dated compared to Gbets — the UI hasn't had a major refresh in a while. That said, it's stable and reliable. Everything loads, bets place correctly, and the live betting section updates in real time. Sometimes boring is good — you don't want flashy design, you want your bet to go through.

WSB's big advantage is their data-free mobile site. If you're tight on data (and let's be real, data prices in SA are still ridiculous), WSB lets you bet without using any mobile data on selected networks. Gbets doesn't offer this.

Casino & Lucky Numbers

Gbets Casino

Gbets has a surprisingly good casino section for a bookmaker that started as sports-only. You'll find slots from top providers, table games (blackjack, roulette, baccarat), and crash games including Aviator and JetX. If you enjoy the occasional casino session between sports bets, Gbets has you covered.

WSB Casino & Lucky Numbers

WSB's casino is more limited — a selection of slots and their popular Lucky Numbers product. Lucky Numbers is WSB's bread and butter though — you pick numbers and bet on lottery draws from around the world (UK, Ireland, Greece, etc.). WSB has one of the best Lucky Numbers offerings in SA, with draws happening every few minutes.

Both offer Lucky Numbers, but WSB edges it with more draw options and slightly better payouts on certain number combinations. If Lucky Numbers is your thing, WSB is the better home.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Gbets or WSB better for PSL betting?

WSB edges it for PSL with more market depth — player props, corner totals, and half-time/full-time combos. Gbets covers the basics well but WSB has been the PSL specialist since the early 2000s.

Which pays out faster — Gbets or WSB?

Gbets, thanks to their Ozow instant withdrawal option. WSB processes via standard EFT which takes 24-48 hours. Both require FICA verification first.

Do Gbets and WSB offer live streaming?

Neither offers comprehensive live streaming. Both have live betting with real-time stats and score updates, but for actual match streaming you'll need a separate service or a bookmaker like Betway.

Can I use both Gbets and WSB at the same time?

Are Gbets and WSB licensed in South Africa?

Yes, both are licensed and regulated by South African provincial gambling boards. Your deposits are protected and payouts are guaranteed by law. Never bet with unlicensed operators.
