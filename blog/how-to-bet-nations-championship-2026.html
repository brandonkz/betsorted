How to Bet on the Nations Championship 2026 — Your Springboks Betting Guide

Updated 22 March 2026 · By BetSorted · 10 min read

International rugby is about to change forever. The Nations Championship — the sport's most ambitious new competition — kicks off in July 2026, and if you're a South African rugby fan, you're probably already planning which matches to watch, which braai to attend, and how to make the most of the Springboks' campaign.

But have you thought about betting on it? The Nations Championship is a tournament-style competition, which means new betting angles that didn't exist with the old mid-year tours. Accumulators across match weekends, outright winner markets, handicap betting — there's a lot to work with.

This guide covers everything an SA punter needs to know: what the Nations Championship actually is, what betting markets to expect, which bookmakers have the best rugby odds, and how to build smart accumulators across the tournament.

What Is the Nations Championship?

Right, let's start with the basics — because this is still new and there's a fair amount of confusion out there.

The Nations Championship replaces the traditional mid-year and end-of-year international rugby tours. Instead of one-off test series (like the old incoming tours to SA or the Boks' November tour to Europe), we now have a structured competition with standings, pools, and a finals series.

Here's the format:

  • 12 teams divided into two pools of six
  • Home and away matches within each pool
  • Cross-pool matches against teams from the other pool
  • A finals series featuring the top teams
  • Promotion and relegation — bottom teams face dropping out, which adds genuine stakes to every match

The participating teams include the Six Nations countries (England, France, Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Italy) and the Rugby Championship nations (South Africa, New Zealand, Australia, Argentina) plus two additional qualifying teams. Exact fixtures and scheduling will be confirmed closer to July, but expect matches spread across the mid-year and end-of-year windows.

For the Springboks, this means every match counts — no more "experimental" squads for November tours. Points matter, standings matter, and the road to the finals runs through every single fixture.

The Springboks' Chances — Can the Boks Win It?

Short answer: absolutely. The Springboks are back-to-back Rugby World Cup champions (2019 and 2023) and arguably the strongest team in world rugby right now. Under Rassie Erasmus, they've built a squad with ridiculous depth — the famous "bomb squad" bench, the ability to rotate players without losing quality, and a winning mentality that's hard to replicate.

Key factors for the Boks in the Nations Championship:

Home advantage is massive. If the Boks play pool matches at Loftus Versfeld, Ellis Park, or Cape Town Stadium, they'll be near-unbeatable. The Springboks' home record over the past five years is exceptional, and the fortress atmosphere at a packed Loftus is worth 10 points on any handicap.

Squad depth matters in a long tournament. This isn't a three-match test series. The Nations Championship runs across multiple weekends, and the Boks' ability to rotate without weakening is a genuine advantage over teams like Ireland or England who rely more heavily on their first XV.

The Rassie factor. Love him or hate him, Erasmus is the best coach in world rugby at preparing teams for specific opponents. In a tournament format where you face different styles (forward-dominated England, running rugby from New Zealand, set-piece battles with France), his tactical flexibility is invaluable.

Nations Championship Betting Markets Explained

If you've bet on rugby before, most of these will be familiar. If you haven't, here's a crash course on the markets you'll find at SA bookmakers for Nations Championship matches:

Match Winner (1X2 or Head-to-Head)

The simplest bet — who wins the match? For rugby, it's usually offered as head-to-head (no draw option, extra time or golden point decides) or 1X2 (including the draw). Most SA bookmakers use head-to-head for rugby.

Example: Springboks vs Ireland — Boks at 1.75, Ireland at 2.10. A R100 bet on the Boks returns R175 if they win.

Handicap (Point Spread)

The bookmaker gives one team a points advantage to even up the odds. This is where rugby betting gets interesting — and where the best value often hides.

Example: Springboks -7.5 vs England at 1.90. The Boks need to win by 8 or more points for your bet to win. This is more challenging than just picking the winner, but the odds reflect that. Handicap markets are brilliant for matches where one team is a heavy favourite and the match winner odds are too short to bother with.

Total Points Over/Under

Will the match produce more or fewer than a set number of points? This is ideal when you have a view on the style of the match but aren't sure who wins.

Example: Total Points Over 45.5 at 1.85. If you think it'll be a fast, open game (say, Boks vs New Zealand), the over could be good value. If you expect a forward-dominated arm-wrestle (Boks vs England at Twickenham in the rain), take the under.

First Try Scorer

Pick who scores the first try. This is the glamour market — long odds, hard to predict, but incredibly satisfying when it comes in. Wingers and outside centres typically score more tries, but forwards can snipe one from a rolling maul.

Example: Cheslin Kolbe to score first try at 8.00. A R50 bet returns R400. Kolbe is always a threat, and at 8.00 there's value if the Boks get early ball wide.

Anytime Try Scorer

Easier than first try scorer — your player just needs to score at any point in the match. Odds are shorter but hit rates are much higher.

Example: Kurt-Lee Arendse anytime try scorer at 2.50. Arendse is one of the most prolific try scorers in world rugby and touches the ball frequently from fullback. At 2.50, this is a solid bet for most Bok matches.

Winning Margin

How much will the winning team win by? Typically banded (1–5, 6–10, 11–15, 16–20, 21+). Pays well if you nail the range.

Half-Time / Full-Time

Who leads at half-time and who wins at full-time? The combination creates interesting odds, especially for matches where you expect a comeback or a team that starts slow.

Outright Tournament Winner

Who wins the whole Nations Championship? This is a long-term market that opens before the tournament and runs until the final. The Boks will likely be among the favourites at 3.00–4.00.

Accumulator Strategies for the Nations Championship

A tournament format like the Nations Championship is tailor-made for accumulators (also called multis or parlays). Instead of betting on isolated test matches, you've got multiple fixtures each weekend that you can combine. Here's how to approach it smartly:

Strategy 1: The Home Banker Acca

Home advantage in international rugby is massive — roughly 60-65% of test matches are won by the home team. Build an accumulator using home teams across the weekend's fixtures. Three home banker selections at 1.50 each gives you a combined acca of 3.38.

Example:

  • Springboks (home) vs Argentina — 1.45
  • France (home) vs Scotland — 1.55
  • New Zealand (home) vs Australia — 1.50

Combined odds: 3.38. A R200 stake returns R676. Not earth-shattering, but a very achievable acca.

Strategy 2: The Handicap Value Acca

When the match winner odds are too short, switch to handicaps. This gives you better odds per leg while still backing the teams you think will dominate.

Example:

  • Springboks -10.5 vs Italy — 1.85
  • Ireland -7.5 vs Scotland — 1.90

Combined odds: 3.52. R150 returns R528. Two legs, two strong teams expected to win comfortably. The handicap gives you proper value.

Strategy 3: The Try Scorer Special

Combine anytime try scorer bets across different matches. This is riskier but pays handsomely. Pick prolific try scorers who are virtually guaranteed to touch the ball frequently.

Example:

  • Cheslin Kolbe anytime try scorer — 2.20
  • Will Jordan (NZ) anytime try scorer — 2.00

Combined odds: 4.40. R100 returns R440. Both are electric attacking players who score regularly. This is the type of acca that makes Saturday afternoon rugby even more exciting.

Strategy 4: The Outright + Match Combo

Back the Springboks to win the tournament outright early at good odds. Then, as the tournament progresses, bet individual matches to either hedge your position or double down. If the Boks start well and their outright odds shorten, you've already locked in value.

Key Fixtures for SA Punters

While the full schedule hasn't been released, here are the matches every South African punter should have circled:

Springboks vs Ireland (Pool Stage)

The big one. Ireland have been the Boks' biggest rivals over the past few years, and this will probably be the match that decides the pool. Expect tight odds — something like Boks 1.90, Ireland 1.90 if it's in SA, or Boks 2.20, Ireland 1.70 if it's in Dublin.

Springboks vs New Zealand

The ultimate rugby rivalry. These two have history, intensity, and genuine animosity that makes every matchup unmissable. Whether it's at Ellis Park or Eden Park, this will be box office. From a betting perspective, expect a tight handicap line — probably -3.5 to the home team.

Springboks vs England

Always a grind, always physical. England under Steve Borthwick play conservative, territory-based rugby. The total points under could be valuable here — these matches tend to be lower-scoring. Under 42.5 points at 1.85 is often where the value sits.

Springboks vs France

France at home are formidable, especially at Stade de France. This could be one of the toughest away fixtures for the Boks. If France are hosting, taking the handicap points (+3.5 or +5.5 for the Boks) might be the smart play.

Live Betting During Matches

The Nations Championship will be brilliant for live (in-play) betting. Rugby matches swing momentum constantly — a yellow card, a dominant scrum, or a breakaway try can shift the game instantly.

Here's what to watch for with live betting:

  • Yellow cards: When a team goes down to 14 men, the opposition's live odds drop significantly. If you think the 14-man team can hold on (the Boks are excellent at this), there can be value in backing them live at inflated odds.
  • Momentum shifts: If the Boks trail at half-time but you know they're a second-half team (they historically are, thanks to the bomb squad impact), the live odds for a Bok win could offer serious value at the break.
  • Next try scorer: Some bookmakers offer next try scorer markets during the match. If you notice a team attacking the blindside channel repeatedly, the winger on that side becomes a strong next try scorer pick.
