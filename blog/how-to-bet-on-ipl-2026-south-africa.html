IPL Betting Markets Explained

The IPL offers a huge variety of betting markets per match. If you're new to cricket betting, or you've only ever bet on match winner, here's a breakdown of the key markets you'll find at SA bookmakers. For a deeper dive, read our full cricket betting guide.

Match Winner

The simplest bet — which team wins the match. In T20 cricket, home advantage is real (roughly 55% win rate) and the toss matters, especially at dew-affected venues. Always consider waiting for the toss before placing this bet.

Top Batsman

Which player scores the most runs in a team's innings (or the match). Openers and top-order batsmen have the most opportunities, but explosive middle-order players like Klaasen can win this from fewer deliveries. Good value often lies with in-form number 3 or 4 batsmen.

Top Bowler

The bowler who takes the most wickets in a team's bowling innings. Death bowlers like Rabada and Nortjé tend to accumulate wickets because batsmen are taking risks. Spinners on turning pitches also deserve attention.

Total Runs (Over/Under)

A combined runs line for the match — for example, over/under 340.5 total runs. This is heavily venue-dependent. High-scoring grounds like Chinnaswamy (Bengaluru) and Wankhede (Mumbai) push you towards the over. Slower pitches in Chennai favour the under.

Total Sixes

How many sixes will be hit in the match. Again, ground size and pitch conditions matter enormously. Smaller boundaries and flat pitches mean more sixes. This market can be profitable if you study venue-specific six-hitting data.

First Wicket Method

How will the first wicket fall — caught, bowled, LBW, run out, or stumped? "Caught" is by far the most common in T20 cricket (typically 60%+), so odds on caught are usually short. LBW can offer value on seaming pitches. Check our glossary for terms you're not sure about.

Most Fours

Which player hits the most boundaries (fours) in the match. Openers and top-order batsmen dominate this market because they face the most balls during the powerplay when the field is up.

Player Performance

A combined points market (runs scored + wickets taken + catches). All-rounders like Marco Jansen and Liam Livingstone are interesting here because they can score points with both bat and ball.

Man of the Match

Predict who'll be named player of the match. This market has long odds and is essentially a punt, but it's fun and can pay handsomely. All-rounders and match-winning performers are your best bets.

Tournament Outright Markets

Beyond individual matches, the IPL offers several tournament-long markets that are available from day one. These are where patient bettors can find real value — especially early in the tournament before form lines are established.

IPL 2026 Winner

Which franchise lifts the trophy? This is the marquee outright bet. Teams with deep squads, experienced coaches, and strong home-ground records tend to go furthest. The IPL uses a playoff format (top 4 qualify), so consistency matters more than one-off brilliance. Early-season odds are typically the most generous.

Top Run Scorer (Orange Cap)

The player who scores the most runs across the entire tournament. Historically, this goes to an opener or number 3 batsman who plays every match. Virat Kohli and Jos Buttler are perennial contenders. From an SA perspective, de Kock is always in the mix if he opens and Lucknow play well enough to go deep.

Top Wicket Taker (Purple Cap)

The tournament's leading wicket-taker. Fast bowlers who bowl both in the powerplay and at the death have the best chance — they get two bites at the cherry. Kagiso Rabada is consistently among the top wicket-takers in the IPL. Yuzvendra Chahal and Rashid Khan are spin options.

Most Sixes in the Tournament

A fun market that tends to go to a top-order power-hitter. Look for players who bat at venues with short boundaries and consistently hit in the arc.

Most Centuries

T20 centuries are rare, so this market is about identifying who's most likely to produce one or two. In-form openers at big-scoring venues are your starting point.

Value alert: Outright markets offer the best value before the tournament starts. Once a player has two good games, their odds halve. If you've done your homework on squad composition and player form, lock in your outrights early.

T20 Betting Strategy Tips for IPL

T20 cricket is the most unpredictable format, which means casual bettors lose money on gut feelings. Here's how to approach IPL betting with an actual strategy:

1. The Toss Is Huge

In evening IPL matches, dew makes the ball slippery for bowlers in the second innings. This means batting second becomes significantly easier at certain venues. At grounds like Wankhede and Chinnaswamy, the team batting second has historically won more often. Our advice: never bet match winner before the toss in evening games. Wait the 30 minutes — it's worth it.

2. Pitch Reports Are Non-Negotiable

Every IPL venue behaves differently. A flat track at the Chinnaswamy can produce 400+ combined runs, while a Chepauk turner might see both teams struggle to 140. Before every bet, check the pitch report. Curator comments, recent match scores at the venue, and the time of year (pitches deteriorate as the tournament progresses) all matter.

3. Powerplay vs Death Bowling

T20 matches have distinct phases. The powerplay (overs 1–6) is when fields are restricted and runs flow. The middle overs (7–15) tend to be slower. The death overs (16–20) are high-scoring and high-wicket chaos. Teams with strong death bowlers (like those with Rabada or Jasprit Bumrah) tend to defend totals better. Factor this into your total runs and match winner bets.

4. Chasing vs Setting — Ground Matters

Some IPL grounds heavily favour chasing (especially under lights with dew), while others are neutral. Mumbai's Wankhede Stadium is famously chase-friendly. Chennai's Chepauk can be easier to bat first on because the pitch deteriorates. Knowing the chasing win percentage at each venue gives you a genuine edge in match winner and total runs markets.

5. Weather in India

The IPL runs from late March to late May — Indian summer. Extreme heat, humidity, and occasional rain all play a role. DLS method applies in rain-affected games, which can affect your bets. Humidity affects dew, which affects second-innings conditions. Keep an eye on weather forecasts, especially for evening games.

6. Team Form vs Player Form

In the IPL, individual match-winners can override team quality. A team struggling at 7th on the table can still beat the table-toppers if one player has a blinder. For match winner bets, look at recent team form. For player prop bets (top batsman, top bowler), focus on individual form, matchups, and venue history. These are two different analyses.

Live Betting on IPL Matches

If there's one format made for in-play betting, it's T20 cricket. Matches last roughly three hours, momentum swings happen every few overs, and the odds move constantly. Live betting on the IPL is where sharp punters make their money.

Why IPL Is Perfect for In-Play

T20 cricket is volatile by design. A team cruising at 80/0 after 10 overs can suddenly lose 4 wickets in 3 overs and collapse. A required run rate of 10 per over looks impossible until a power-hitter smashes 25 off one over. These swings create value in the live odds that pre-match bets simply can't capture.

Key Live Betting Moments

  • Post-toss: Match winner odds shift significantly after the toss at dew-affected venues. This is your first live betting opportunity.
  • End of powerplay: After 6 overs, you have a clear picture of the batting team's intent and the pitch behaviour. Adjust your total runs expectations.
  • Fall of key wickets: When a set batsman gets out, the batting team's projected total drops. If you think the remaining batsmen can recover, there's value in the over.
  • Death overs (16–20): The most volatile phase. Runs per over can be 6 or 20. If a team needs 50 off 30 balls, the live odds on a chase can swing dramatically over by over.
  • Start of the chase: If the first innings total is higher/lower than expected, the chasing team's odds reset. A target of 200 on a flat pitch might have the chasing team as favourites despite the big total.

Building IPL Accumulators

Accumulators (multis) combine multiple selections into one bet with compounding odds. The IPL's packed schedule — often with double-header days — makes it perfect for cross-match accumulators. But there's a right way and a wrong way to do it.

How IPL Accumulators Work

You combine selections from different matches into one bet. For example:

🎯 Example IPL Accumulator

Leg 1: Mumbai Indians to beat Chennai Super Kings @ 1.90

Leg 2: Kagiso Rabada top bowler (Punjab Kings) @ 3.50

Leg 3: Over 160.5 first innings runs (Delhi Capitals vs RCB) @ 1.85

Combined odds: 12.29 | R100 bet returns R1,229

That R100 bet turning into R1,229 is the appeal. But remember — all three legs must win. One loss and your entire bet is gone.

IPL Accumulator Tips

  • Keep it short: 2–3 legs maximum. T20 cricket is too unpredictable for 6 or 7-leg accas. Each additional leg dramatically reduces your probability of winning.
  • Mix market types: Don't just stack match winners. Combine a match winner with a player prop and a total runs line. This diversifies your risk across different aspects of the game.
  • Avoid heavy favourites: Stacking 1.30 odds across 5 legs gives you combined odds of about 3.70 — and if one favourite loses, you get nothing. It's usually better to take 2 well-researched legs at better prices.
  • Double-header days are gold: When there are two IPL matches in a day, you can build a same-day accumulator and know your result by evening.

How to Calculate Your Returns

Before placing any IPL bet, you should know exactly what you stand to win. Sounds obvious, but many punters don't bother — and then they're surprised by the payout (or lack thereof).

Simple Calculation

For a single bet with decimal odds:

Returns = Stake × Odds

Example: You bet R200 on Gujarat Titans to win at odds of 2.10.

Returns = R200 × 2.10 = R420 (that's R220 profit + your R200 stake back).

Accumulator Calculation

For accumulators, multiply all the odds together, then multiply by your stake:

Example: Three legs at 1.90, 3.50, and 1.85.

Combined odds = 1.90 × 3.50 × 1.85 = 12.30

R100 stake × 12.30 = R1,230 total returns (R1,130 profit).
