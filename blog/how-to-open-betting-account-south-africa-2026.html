How to Open a Betting Account in South Africa: Complete 2026 Guide

Published March 10, 2026 • 9 min read • Brandon Katz

Opening a betting account in South Africa is straightforward, but there are a few things you need to know before you start — especially around FICA verification, which trips up a lot of first-time bettors. This guide walks you through the entire process, from choosing a bookmaker to placing your first bet.

Whether you're a complete beginner wanting to bet on the PSL weekend fixtures or someone moving from retail branches to online betting, this covers everything step by step.

Before You Start: Requirements

To open a legal betting account in South Africa, you need:

  • Be 18 or older — It's illegal to gamble under 18 in SA. No exceptions.
  • South African ID or valid passport — For FICA identity verification
  • Proof of address — Bank statement, utility bill, or similar document (not older than 3 months)
  • A bank account or payment method — For deposits and withdrawals
  • An email address and phone number — For account registration and verification

⚠️ Important: All licensed South African bookmakers are required by law to verify your identity before you can withdraw funds. This is called FICA (Financial Intelligence Centre Act) verification. It's not optional, and it applies to every bookmaker — Betway, Hollywoodbets, Sportingbet, all of them.

Register Online

Visit your chosen bookmaker's website and look for the "Sign Up", "Register", or "Join" button — it's usually in the top-right corner. You'll need to provide:

  • Full name (as it appears on your ID)
  • South African ID number or passport number
  • Date of birth
  • Email address
  • Phone number (South African mobile number)
  • Physical address
  • Choose a username and password

The form takes about 3–5 minutes. Double-check that your name and ID number match your actual documents — mismatches cause FICA delays later.

Complete FICA Verification

FICA is the part that catches people off guard. After registration, you'll need to upload:

  • Identity document: Clear photo/scan of your SA ID book, smart ID card, or passport
  • Proof of address: Bank statement, municipal bill, or phone bill dated within the last 3 months

Most bookmakers let you upload these directly through their website or app. Some tips to avoid delays:

  • Take photos in good lighting — blurry documents get rejected
  • Make sure all four corners of the document are visible
  • The name on your proof of address must match your registration details
  • A bank statement downloaded as PDF from your banking app works perfectly

Processing time: Verification takes anywhere from a few minutes (Betway is fast) to 24–48 hours, depending on the bookmaker. Some allow you to bet immediately but require FICA before your first withdrawal.

Place Your First Bet

With money in your account, navigate to the sport you want to bet on. Here's the basic process:

  1. Select your sport (e.g., Football → PSL)
  2. Find the match you want to bet on
  3. Click on the odds for your selection (e.g., "Orlando Pirates to win" at 1.85)
  4. Your selection appears in the bet slip
  5. Enter your stake (the amount you want to bet)
  6. The potential payout is calculated automatically — or use our betting calculator

That's it. If your selection wins, the payout is credited to your account balance automatically.

Understanding FICA: Why It Matters

FICA stands for the Financial Intelligence Centre Act. It's South African law that requires financial institutions — including betting companies — to verify the identity of their customers. This is primarily about preventing money laundering and underage gambling.

Every legal SA bookmaker must comply with FICA. There's no way around it. If a site lets you bet and withdraw without any verification, it's either operating illegally or it's an offshore site without SA licensing — both of which put your money at risk.

What FICA means for you in practice:

  • You need to verify once per bookmaker — after that, deposits and withdrawals work normally
  • Verification is required before your first withdrawal (some bookmakers require it before the first deposit)
  • Your documents are handled under POPIA (Protection of Personal Information Act) — they can't share your data
  • If you change addresses, you may need to re-verify

Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Choosing an Unlicensed Bookmaker

Stick with bookmakers licensed by a South African provincial gambling board. These include Betway, Hollywoodbets, Sportingbet, Supabets, Easybet, World Sports Betting, Sunbet, Betfred, and others listed on our bookmakers page. Offshore sites might accept SA customers, but you have zero legal protection if something goes wrong.

2. Ignoring the Welcome Bonus Terms

Welcome bonuses sound great — and they can be — but always read the wagering requirements. A R5,000 bonus with 10x wagering means you need to bet R50,000 before you can withdraw bonus winnings. Use our free bets guide to understand how bonus terms work.

3. Not Setting a Budget

Before you bet a single rand, decide how much you're willing to lose in a week or month. This isn't being pessimistic — it's being smart. All bookmakers offer deposit limits and self-exclusion tools. Use them. Read our responsible gambling guide.

4. Betting Without Research

Don't just pick teams you like. Check form, head-to-head records, injuries, and odds comparison. Our blog covers betting tips for PSL, rugby, cricket, and more.

5. Chasing Losses

Lost your bet? The worst thing you can do is immediately place a bigger bet to "win it back." This is how betting becomes a problem. Stick to your budget and walk away if you're on a losing streak.

Understanding the Basics of Betting

If you're completely new to sports betting, here are the fundamentals:

How Odds Work

South African bookmakers use decimal odds. If the odds are 2.50, a R100 bet returns R250 (your R100 stake + R150 profit). Simple maths:

Potential payout = Stake × Odds

Common Bet Types

  • Single bet: One selection. Simplest bet type. If it wins, you win.
  • Accumulator (multi): Multiple selections combined. All must win for you to get paid, but the odds multiply. Use our accumulator calculator.
  • Each way: Your selection wins OR places (top 2–3). Common in horse racing. See our each-way calculator.
  • Handicap: One team is given a virtual head start. Useful when one team is heavily favoured.
  • Over/Under: Bet on whether the total goals/points will be above or below a set number.

Betting Tax in South Africa

Good news: in South Africa, gambling winnings are generally not taxed for recreational bettors. The bookmaker pays a turnover tax, not you. However, if betting is your primary source of income, SARS may consider your winnings taxable. For most casual punters, this isn't a concern.

Frequently Asked Questions

How old do I need to be to bet in South Africa?
You must be 18 years or older. All licensed bookmakers verify your age through FICA. Underage gambling is illegal and bookmakers can face penalties for allowing it.
What documents do I need for FICA?
A South African ID (smart card, ID book, or passport) and proof of address (bank statement, utility bill, or municipal account dated within the last 3 months).
How long does FICA verification take?
It varies by bookmaker. Betway can verify within minutes. Others take 24–48 hours. Submitting clear, legible documents speeds up the process.
What is the minimum deposit to start betting?
Most SA bookmakers accept deposits from R5–R20. Supabets starts at R5, while Betway and Hollywoodbets start at R10. Betfred's minimum is R20.
Can I have accounts with multiple bookmakers?
Yes, absolutely. Many experienced bettors maintain 2–3 accounts to compare odds and use different promotions. You can only have one account per bookmaker, though.
Do I pay tax on betting winnings?
Recreational bettors generally don't pay tax on winnings in South Africa. The bookmaker pays a turnover tax on your behalf. If betting is your primary income, different rules may apply.
What if I want to close my betting account?
All licensed bookmakers offer self-exclusion options. You can temporarily suspend or permanently close your account by contacting customer support. Some bookmakers also allow you to set cooling-off periods through their app or website.
