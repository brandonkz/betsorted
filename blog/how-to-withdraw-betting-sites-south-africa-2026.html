How to Withdraw from Betting Sites in South Africa 2026

You've hit a nice winner on the weekend's PSL action. Chiefs finally came through at decent odds and now you want that cash in your bank account. But how long is it actually going to take? And what hoops do you need to jump through?

The Golden Rule: Complete FICA First

Before any bookmaker in South Africa will let you withdraw, you need to complete FICA verification. This isn't optional — it's the law under the Financial Intelligence Centre Act. Every licensed SA bookmaker must verify your identity.

Here's what you'll need:

  • South African ID document — green barcoded ID book, smart ID card, or passport
  • Proof of address — utility bill, bank statement, or municipal account (dated within 3 months)
  • Proof of banking — some bookmakers also need a bank statement or screenshot showing your account number and name

Pro tip: do this the moment you register, not when you're trying to cash out a big win at 11 PM on a Saturday. Most bookmakers verify within a few hours during business days, but some can take 24–48 hours. Get it done early and you'll never have to wait when the money matters.

Withdrawal Methods Available in South Africa

SA bookmakers offer several ways to get your money out. Here's what each one actually means:

Standard EFT (Bank Transfer)

The most common withdrawal method. Your winnings are transferred directly to your bank account via the standard EFT banking system. Processing time varies wildly — from instant (Betway) to 48 hours (some smaller bookmakers). No fees at most bookmakers. Works with all major SA banks: FNB, Standard Bank, Nedbank, Absa, Capitec, and others.

Instant EFT

Instant EFT is the holy grail. The money hits your account within minutes of requesting. Betway is the gold standard here — their instant EFT is genuinely instant. Some other bookmakers offer "fast EFT" which is quicker than standard but not truly instant. If fast payouts matter to you (and they should), this is what you want.

Ozow

Ozow is primarily a deposit method, but some bookmakers also use it for withdrawals. It connects directly to your online banking and processes payments quickly. If your bookmaker supports Ozow withdrawals, it's typically faster than standard EFT.

Capitec Pay

If you bank with Capitec — and let's be honest, a massive chunk of SA bettors do — Capitec Pay is a convenient option. Several bookmakers now support Capitec Pay for both deposits and withdrawals, with processing times generally faster than standard EFT.

FNB & Standard Bank Direct Transfer

Some bookmakers offer direct bank transfer options specifically for FNB and Standard Bank customers. These can be faster than generic EFT processing, especially if the bookmaker banks with the same institution.

Step-by-Step: How to Withdraw from Any SA Betting Site

The process is basically the same at every bookmaker, with minor UI differences:

Step 1: Make Sure You're FICA Verified

Log into your account and check your verification status. If you haven't submitted your ID and proof of address yet, do it now. You literally cannot withdraw without this.

Step 2: Navigate to the Withdrawal Section

Usually found under "My Account" → "Withdraw" or "Banking" → "Withdraw." On mobile apps, it's typically in the menu under your account settings.

Step 3: Choose Your Withdrawal Method

Select EFT, Instant EFT, or whatever method your bookmaker supports. Make sure your banking details are correct — the account must be in your name.

Step 4: Enter the Amount

Enter how much you want to withdraw. Stay above the minimum (see our table above). Most bookmakers don't have maximum withdrawal limits for standard amounts, but very large wins (R100,000+) might need manual processing.

Step 5: Confirm and Wait

Submit the request. With Betway, check your bank account in a few minutes. With everyone else, check back in the timeframe we listed above. If it takes longer than expected, contact customer support — don't just wait and hope.

Why Your Withdrawal Might Be Delayed

Before you rage-tweet at your bookmaker's customer service, here are the common reasons withdrawals get held up:

  • FICA not completed — by far the most common issue. Submit your documents before trying to withdraw.
  • Bonus wagering requirements not met — if you claimed a welcome bonus, you might need to wager a certain amount before withdrawing. Check the bonus T&Cs.
  • Banking details mismatch — the bank account must be in the same name as your betting account. No exceptions.
  • Large withdrawal amounts — wins above R50,000–R100,000 may trigger additional security checks. This is normal and required by law.
  • Weekend/public holiday processing — some bookmakers don't process withdrawals on weekends. Betway is the notable exception.
  • First-time withdrawal — your very first withdrawal often takes longer as the bookmaker does additional verification checks.

Which Banks Work Best for Betting Withdrawals?

All major SA banks work with all licensed bookmakers. But some combinations are faster than others:

Capitec — the most popular bank among SA bettors. Deposits and withdrawals are seamless with most bookmakers. Capitec Pay makes it even faster at supported sites.

FNB — great integration with most bookmakers. FNB's instant payment system means you often receive withdrawals faster than other banks.

Standard Bank, Absa, Nedbank — all work perfectly well. Standard EFT timing applies. No issues to report.

African Bank, TymeBank, Discovery Bank — these work too, but we've seen occasional delays compared to the Big Five. Nothing dramatic — maybe an extra few hours on standard EFT.

Withdrawal Tips from Someone Who's Done It 100+ Times

After years of betting in South Africa, here's what I've learned about getting your money out quickly:

  • Complete FICA on day one. Seriously. Don't wait until you have a R5,000 payout sitting there. Do it when you register and you'll never think about it again.
  • Keep your banking details updated. If you switch banks, update your betting account immediately. Nothing worse than a withdrawal bouncing because your details are outdated.
  • Withdraw regularly. Don't let large balances sit in your betting account. Take profits out. It's good bankroll management and it means your withdrawal process stays "warm" — your bookmaker knows your banking details work.
  • Screenshot everything. Take screenshots of your withdrawal requests. If there's ever a dispute, you have evidence of when you requested the payout.
  • Separate speed from signup intent. Betway remains a useful manual comparison point for fast payouts, but BetSorted should route generic signup intent toward Hollywoodbets while that tracked partnership is live.

What to Do If Your Withdrawal Is Stuck

It happens. Here's your action plan:

  1. Wait the stated timeframe. If your bookmaker says 24–48 hours, don't panic at the 25-hour mark.
  2. Check your FICA status. Log in and confirm your account is fully verified. This is the #1 reason for stuck withdrawals.
  3. Contact live chat. Every major SA bookmaker has live chat. Use it. Email is slower and less effective.
  4. Ask for a reference number. When you contact support, get a reference number for your withdrawal request. This creates accountability.
  5. Escalate if needed. If it's been more than 72 hours with no resolution, you can lodge a complaint with the relevant provincial gambling board. This is rare, but knowing the option exists keeps bookmakers honest.

FAQ

How long does it take to withdraw from betting sites in South Africa?

It depends on the bookmaker and payment method. Betway offers instant EFT withdrawals (under 5 minutes). Hollywoodbets and Sportingbet process within 1–24 hours. Others like Supabets, 10bet, and Playabets typically take 24–48 hours. E-wallets and Ozow can speed things up at some bookmakers.

Do South African betting sites charge withdrawal fees?

Most SA bookmakers don't charge withdrawal fees for standard EFT payments. However, some may charge for expedited methods or if you haven't met minimum wagering requirements. Always check the T&Cs — Betway, Hollywoodbets, Sportingbet, and 10bet all offer fee-free EFT withdrawals.

What is FICA verification and why do I need it to withdraw?

FICA (Financial Intelligence Centre Act) requires all SA bookmakers to verify your identity before processing withdrawals. You'll need to submit a copy of your South African ID or passport, plus proof of address (utility bill or bank statement dated within 3 months). This is a legal requirement — no bookmaker can skip it.

Can I withdraw to a different bank account than the one I deposited from?

Generally no. Most SA bookmakers require that withdrawals go to the same bank account used for deposits, and the account must be in your name. This is an anti-money-laundering requirement. If you need to change your banking details, contact customer support — they'll ask for additional verification.

What's the minimum withdrawal amount at SA betting sites?

Minimum withdrawals vary by bookmaker. Betway has a R10 minimum, Hollywoodbets is R50, Sportingbet is R50, Supabets is R50, and most others are between R10 and R100. Playabets has one of the lowest at R10. Check our comparison table above for the full breakdown.
