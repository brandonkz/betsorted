Lottostar vs GBets South Africa 2026: Lucky Numbers, Odds & Sports Compared

Published April 12, 2026 • 9 min read • Brandon Katz

Lottostar and GBets are two South African-born bookmakers with loyal followings — but they've built their brands on almost completely different products. Lottostar has cemented its position as the home of Lucky Numbers in South Africa, with more lottery draws available than any other bookmaker in the country. GBets has gone in the opposite direction, positioning itself as the punter's sports bookmaker — strong on PSL, rugby, and local sports culture, with a loyal community that punches above its weight.

Both are legitimate, licensed, and genuinely popular. The question is which one fits your betting habits. This comparison looks at Lucky Numbers depth, sports odds quality, withdrawal speed, mobile experience, and the overall value proposition for each. Lucky Numbers: Lottostar's Home Territory

If Lucky Numbers is a regular part of your betting week — and for a huge portion of South African punters, it absolutely is — this section tells you most of what you need to know.

Lottostar is the dominant force in SA Lucky Numbers betting. They offer over 50 daily draws from both South African and international lotteries. We're talking UK 49s (multiple draws per day), the SA National Lottery, Powerball, various international draws, and more. The sheer volume of draws means there's always something to bet on at any hour. Whether you're a morning punter doing the 10h30 draw or a late-night player chasing the 22h00 results, Lottostar has options that no other SA bookmaker comes close to matching.

The bet types available are comprehensive — two-ball, three-ball, four-ball, five-ball, and combinations. Payouts are clearly displayed before you place, so there's no ambiguity about what you stand to win. For serious Lucky Numbers punters who have specific draws and bet types they follow religiously, Lottostar's depth is simply unmatched in South Africa. This is what their platform was built for, and it shows.

GBets offers Lucky Numbers too, but it's a supporting act rather than the main event. They have a selection of draws available, but the range is significantly smaller than Lottostar's 50+. For casual Lucky Numbers punters who just want to throw a quick R20 on the UK 49s while they're on the GBets platform for a PSL bet, that's fine. For dedicated Lucky Numbers players who want maximum draw variety, GBets falls short.

Lucky Numbers verdict: Lottostar by a country mile. 50+ daily draws vs GBets' limited selection isn't close. If you're a dedicated Lucky Numbers punter, Lottostar is your home. Also check our guide to best SA bookmakers for Lucky Numbers for the full rankings.

Odds and Sports Depth: GBets Takes the Lead

Flip the Lucky Numbers comparison around and you get the sports betting picture. GBets is the stronger sports platform, and it's not particularly close when you dig into the depth and pricing.

PSL Football

GBets has worked hard to build a reputation as the local punter's bookmaker for SA football. Their PSL odds are competitive, and they cover the full range of Betway Premiership matches with solid pre-match markets. More importantly, they feel invested in the local product — you'll find promotions around big PSL derbies, enhanced odds on Chiefs vs Pirates, and the kind of local market intelligence that suggests they're actually following the league closely rather than just licensing a data feed.

Lottostar covers PSL games — match result, over/under, basic markets — but it's clearly secondary to their numbers product. The market depth is limited compared to GBets, and you won't find the same range of specials or PSL-specific promotions. If football is your main sport and you're betting on multiple PSL matches per weekend, GBets will give you more to work with.

Rugby and the Springboks

This is where GBets really distinguishes itself. Rugby is genuinely important to GBets — they cover the Springboks, URC (Vodacom United Rugby Championship), Currie Cup, and other SA rugby competitions with real depth. Handicap betting, total points, first try scorer, and various props are available on Springbok Tests and big URC fixtures. During a Lions series or Rugby Championship, GBets is one of the better SA bookmakers for rugby market depth.

Lottostar's rugby coverage is basic. Match result and maybe a handicap line on big games — that's about it. Rugby fans will find very little reason to use Lottostar over a dedicated sports bookmaker.

Cricket

GBets covers cricket reasonably well — Proteas matches in major series, the SA20 league, and big ICC tournaments. Not the deepest cricket coverage in SA (that would be Betway or Hollywoodbets), but serviceable for the SA cricket fan. Lottostar offers some cricket on major events but with limited market depth. Neither is the destination of choice for serious cricket bettors, but GBets has a clear edge here too.

Welcome Bonuses

GBets typically runs a R100 bonus offer for new sign-ups, though the exact promotion varies — they update their welcome offer periodically, so always check the current terms on their site before registering. Their minimum deposit is R10, making it accessible for anyone to get started.

Lottostar's welcome offer is a R50 free bet, with a R20 minimum deposit. The free bet is straightforward to use on their Lucky Numbers product or on sports markets. It's not the biggest offer in the SA market, but it's clean and doesn't come loaded with complex wagering requirements.

Neither bonus will make you rich, and neither should be your primary reason for choosing a bookmaker. The ongoing product quality — and for these two platforms, that means Lucky Numbers depth vs sports betting quality — matters far more than a one-time sign-up incentive. Both welcome offers are decent for getting started with minimal risk.

Withdrawal Speed and Payments

Both Lottostar and GBets process EFT withdrawals in the standard 24–48 hour window. This is consistent with the SA market average — not as fast as Betway's near-instant payouts, but nothing unusual. Neither platform charges withdrawal fees, which is always a positive.

For deposits, both accept standard South African banking options — EFT and online banking transfers. GBets' minimum deposit of R10 edges slightly lower than Lottostar's R20, which makes a difference for punters who want to start with a small amount to test the platform.

As with any SA bookmaker, make sure you complete your FICA verification before requesting your first withdrawal. Submit your ID document and proof of address early — it's a standard requirement for all licensed SA bookmakers and avoids any delays when you actually want to access your winnings.

Mobile Experience

Both platforms offer Android apps and mobile-optimised websites. Neither currently has a dedicated iOS app, but both work reasonably well in mobile browser on iPhone.

Lottostar's mobile app is clearly built around the Lucky Numbers product. Navigating to specific draws, placing bets quickly, and checking results is smooth and intuitive. For their core product, the mobile experience is well-designed. The sports betting section on mobile is functional but less polished than the numbers product — it feels like an afterthought compared to the main event.

GBets' mobile app reflects their sports-first focus. Navigating PSL markets, building multi-bets, and tracking live scores is straightforward. The app performs well for pre-match betting and adequately for live betting, though their in-play product isn't as deep as the biggest SA operators. The loyalty points system is accessible through the app, which adds a nice layer of engagement for regular users.

If you're primarily a Lucky Numbers bettor, Lottostar's app is the more refined experience for your specific needs. Sports bettors will prefer GBets' interface. In both cases, mobile data usage is reasonable — neither app is unusually heavy.

GBets' Loyalty Programme: A Genuine Differentiator

One area where GBets stands out beyond just odds and markets is their loyalty programme. Regular punters earn points on bets placed, which accumulate and can be redeemed for bonuses and free bets. The scheme rewards consistent engagement — the more you use the platform, the more value you extract from it over time.

GBets also runs regular sports-specific promotions. Big PSL derbies often come with enhanced odds or deposit bonuses. Springbok Tests are typically accompanied by special offers. This kind of ongoing promotion activity keeps the platform fresh and gives loyal users a reason to keep coming back beyond just the base odds.

Lottostar's ongoing promotions are more focused on their numbers product — bonus draws, enhanced payouts on specific lottery events, and occasional welcome-back offers for inactive accounts. There isn't an equivalent loyalty points scheme for regular bettors.

The Verdict: Lottostar vs GBets South Africa 2026

These two platforms have almost zero overlap in terms of their primary strengths, which makes this comparison more straightforward than most.

If Lucky Numbers is central to your betting — and South Africa has a massive Lucky Numbers market, so statistically there's a good chance it is — then Lottostar is your platform. Nobody else in SA offers 50+ daily draws, and that depth is genuinely valuable for serious numbers punters who want to bet on specific draws throughout the day.

If sports betting is your thing — PSL, rugby, cricket, accumulators, live markets — then GBets is the better choice. They're built for the SA sports punter, their local odds are competitive, their rugby coverage is solid, and the loyalty programme gives regular bettors ongoing value beyond just the base product.

The obvious move for any serious SA bettor is to have accounts with both. Use Lottostar as your Lucky Numbers home and GBets as your sports platform. The two complement each other neatly, and having both costs you nothing.

FAQ: Lottostar vs GBets South Africa

Which has more Lucky Numbers draws — Lottostar or GBets?

Lottostar wins this category decisively. They offer 50+ daily draws from SA and international lotteries, making them the market leader in SA for Lucky Numbers variety. GBets offers Lucky Numbers but with a significantly smaller draw selection. If Lucky Numbers is your primary betting product, Lottostar is the better platform.

Is GBets better than Lottostar for PSL betting?

Yes. GBets is the stronger sports betting platform of the two. They're designed as a sports-first bookmaker with competitive PSL odds, deep rugby coverage, and a strong SA sports betting culture. Lottostar offers PSL betting but it's secondary to their Lucky Numbers product. For PSL and rugby, GBets is the better choice.

What is Lottostar's welcome bonus?

Lottostar offers a R50 free bet as their welcome bonus. This can be used on Lucky Numbers or sports markets. Their minimum deposit is R20. Always check the current terms on the Lottostar website as promotions can change.

Does GBets have a loyalty programme?

Yes. GBets runs a loyalty points system that rewards regular punters. As you bet, you accumulate points that can be redeemed for bonuses and free bets. They also run regular promotions tied to big SA sporting events — Springbok Tests, PSL derbies, and major local sporting occasions are typical examples. Their community engagement and local market focus is one of GBets' genuine strengths.

Are Lottostar and GBets licensed in South Africa?

Yes, both are South African-born bookmakers with valid provincial gambling licences. Both require FICA verification — your SA ID and proof of address — before processing withdrawals. They're legal, regulated, and operate under the same framework as other major SA bookmakers.
