What Is Matched Betting?

Matched betting is a technique that extracts value from bookmaker promotions — sign-up bonuses, free bets, and reload offers — by placing opposing bets to cover every possible outcome. You back a result with one bookmaker and lay the same result at a betting exchange (or use a second bookmaker offering different odds).

The result? A small qualifying loss on your first bet, followed by a guaranteed profit when you use the free bet. Net effect: you pocket most of the bonus value regardless of the sporting outcome.

The Two-Step Process

Step 1: Qualifying Bet

Place a real-money bet to unlock the free bet bonus. Cover the opposite outcome with a second bookmaker. Small loss expected (R10-R30 typically).

Step 2: Free Bet

Use your free bet on one outcome, cover the opposite again. This time you're playing with the bookmaker's money. Profit locked in either way.

Is Matched Betting Legal in South Africa?

Yes, 100%. Matched betting is completely legal in South Africa. You're placing bets with licensed bookmakers regulated by provincial gambling boards. There's no law against using promotions strategically — you're simply being a smart customer.

The only risk? Bookmakers might limit your account if they notice you're only signing up for bonuses. They can't take back legitimate winnings, but they can restrict your future betting. We'll cover how to avoid gubbing later in this guide.

Step-by-Step Example: Betway R2,000 Bonus

Let's walk through a real example using Betway's welcome offer. You deposit R2,000, and Betway matches it with R2,000 in bonus funds. The wagering requirement is 6x at minimum odds of 1.40.

Phase 1: Clear the Wagering (Qualifying Bets)

You need to wager R12,000 total (R2,000 × 6) before you can withdraw. The strategy: bet on strong favourites on Betway, and cover the opposite outcome on another bookmaker.

Example: Sundowns vs Chiefs (PSL)

  • Betway: Back Sundowns to win at 1.45 — stake R2,000 → returns R2,900 if Sundowns win
  • Hollywoodbets: Back Draw + Chiefs (double chance) at 2.20 — stake R1,350 → returns R2,970 if Sundowns don't win
  • Outcome: Sundowns win → +R900 on Betway, -R1,350 on Hollywood = -R450. OR Sundowns don't win → -R2,000 on Betway, +R1,620 on Hollywood = -R380
  • Qualifying loss: R380-R450 (this is the cost of unlocking R2,000 in bonus funds)

Repeat across 6 qualifying bets at R2,000 each. Total qualifying loss: roughly R300-R500 (it varies based on odds matching). You've now cleared the wagering requirement.

Phase 2: Extract the Bonus

Your R2,000 bonus is now withdrawable (or can be bet freely). Place one final matched bet to convert it to cash. If Betway pays out as a free bet (stake not returned), you'll extract roughly 70-80% of the bonus value.

💰 Bottom Line: Deposit R2,000, go through the process, walk away with roughly R600-R800 in profit after covering all outcomes. Zero risk on the sporting result.

The Betting Exchange Problem in South Africa

In the UK, matched bettors use exchanges like Betfair to lay bets (bet against an outcome). South Africa doesn't have licensed betting exchanges, which makes traditional matched betting slightly trickier.

The workaround: Use two regular bookmakers. Back one outcome on the bookmaker with the bonus, and back the opposite outcome(s) on another bookmaker. It's not as precise as using an exchange (you'll lose slightly more on qualifying bets), but it absolutely works.

Some bettors use international exchanges like Betfair (accessible from SA), though you'll need to fund them via international payment methods. For beginners, sticking with two local bookmakers is simpler.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting to place the lay/cover bet — this defeats the entire purpose
  • Miscalculating stakes — always double-check with a calculator
  • Ignoring wagering requirements — withdrawing before you've met rollover means losing the bonus
  • Using the same device/IP for multiple accounts — bookmakers will flag this
  • Betting on obscure markets — stick to popular events with tight odds (PSL, EPL, rugby)
  • Getting emotionally involved — it doesn't matter who wins. You're running a process, not supporting a team
