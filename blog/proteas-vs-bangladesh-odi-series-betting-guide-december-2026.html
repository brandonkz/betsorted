Proteas vs Bangladesh ODI Series Betting Guide: 1 to 7 December 2026

Published: 23 November 2026 • Category: Cricket Betting • Reading time: ~8 minutes

Series details: South Africa vs Bangladesh, three ODIs. 1st ODI: Tuesday 1 December, Buffalo Park, East London. 2nd ODI: Friday 4 December, St George's Park, Gqeberha. 3rd ODI: Monday 7 December, Newlands, Cape Town. CSA lists all three at 13:30 SAST. Confirm times on cricket.co.za before you bet.

The Test series ends this week in Centurion. Then the white-ball leg starts. Bangladesh stay on for three ODIs in the Eastern and Western Cape, followed by three T20Is from 10 to 13 December.

The Proteas won the first Test at the Wanderers by nine wickets. Simon Harmer took six in the second innings and Kagiso Rabada was player of the match. Bangladesh still made 400 in their first innings, their best Test total in South Africa. That is worth remembering. They can bat.

This guide covers the three venues, the markets that suit a home ODI series, and how to compare prices across SA bookmakers. We do not publish odds. Prices move with team news and the weather, and we only quote figures we can check. Look at the live price on the bookmaker you plan to use.

Why this series matters

South Africa co-host the 2027 ODI World Cup. Every home ODI between now and then doubles as a trial. Selectors want to see who can bat through the middle overs and who can bowl at the death on South African pitches. Expect rotation, new faces and some experiments with the batting order.

That matters for betting. A settled first-choice side is easy to price. An experimental side is not. When the squad lands, look for three things: who opens, who keeps wicket and how many frontline spinners are picked. Each one shifts the player markets more than the match result.

Temba Bavuma is out of the second Test with a calf injury. Do not assume he plays the ODIs. Wait for the official CSA squad and the team sheet at the toss.

The three venues

Buffalo Park, East London (1 December)

Buffalo Park is a small ground by international standards. Short boundaries help batters clear the rope. East London also gets wind off the sea, which can help one end and hurt the other. Check the forecast on the morning of the match, not the day before.

St George's Park, Gqeberha (4 December)

St George's Park has a reputation for slower surfaces than the Highveld grounds. Spinners tend to get more out of it than at the Wanderers or Centurion. It is also a windy city. A slow pitch plus wind usually means a lower total, so look at the innings runs line with care.

Newlands, Cape Town (7 December)

Newlands closes the series on a Monday. The ground often favours seam early, then flattens out. The southeaster can blow hard in December. If the series is still alive at 1-1, the third ODI will draw the most money, and prices tend to tighten on popular selections.

A 13:30 start means the side batting second chases into the evening. Dew is less of a factor at a day match than at a floodlit one, but the light and the wind still change across the day. Check the toss before you bet any innings market.

The match winner market

At home, the Proteas will usually be priced as clear favourites against Bangladesh. That makes the match winner short. A short price is not a bad bet by itself, but it needs to be too long for its real chance. Take a made-up example. If a price of 1.30 is on offer and you think the Proteas win 80% of the time, the bet has value. If you think they win 70% of the time, it does not.

ODI match winner markets settle on the official result. That includes results decided by Duckworth-Lewis-Stern after rain. A tie or a no result is handled under each bookmaker's own rules. Read them before you bet.

Series result and correct series score

Series markets let you back a 3-0, 2-1 or other outcome before the first ball. Correct series score pays more than series winner because it is harder to hit. The risk is simple: one rain-affected game or one strong Bangladesh batting day breaks the bet.

If you like the Proteas but think Bangladesh will steal one game, a 2-1 correct score covers that view. If you want to back a whitewash, compare the 3-0 price with what a treble of three single match winner bets would pay. Our accumulator calculator does the maths. The series market is sometimes better, sometimes worse. Check both.

Runs markets

Innings runs lines are set before the toss and move after it. The bookmaker knows the venue averages. Your edge, if you have one, comes from late information: the pitch report, the wind and the final XI.

  • First innings runs: Usually the deepest runs market. Compare the line at three bookmakers. A five-run gap is common and it matters.
  • Runs in the first 10 overs: Powerplay markets suit punters who watch the toss. A new-ball bowler on a seaming Newlands morning changes this line fast.
  • Highest opening partnership: A simple head-to-head between the two openers' stands. It is volatile. Stake small.

Player markets

Top batter and top bowler markets pay well because there are 11 players to choose from. They also lose more often. Three rules help:

  • Wait for the XI. Most bookmakers void top batter bets on players who do not take part. Some settle them as losers. Read the rules.
  • Back the top order for top batter. Numbers one to four face the most balls. That is why they win this market most often.
  • Match the bowler to the venue. A spinner at St George's Park has a better shot at top bowler than the same player at Buffalo Park.

Bangladesh's batters looked solid in the Wanderers Test. Towhid Hridoy made 137 and Litton Das made 120. A touring batter priced long in the top batter market is not always a bad bet. Check the price against how often he has top-scored, not against his name.

Avoid the cricket accumulator trap

A Proteas win at home looks like a safe leg to add to a weekend multi. It is not free. A short leg adds little to the payout and still carries risk. One rain-affected game or one bad batting collapse kills the whole slip. If you want a multi, run it through the accumulator calculator first and compare it with singles. How we would approach it: Wait for the CSA squad and the XI at the toss. Check the wind and pitch report for each venue. Pick one market per match. Compare the price at three bookmakers and take the best number. Set your stake before the first ball and stick to it.

When is the Proteas vs Bangladesh ODI series?
The three ODIs are on Tuesday 1 December at Buffalo Park in East London, Friday 4 December at St George's Park in Gqeberha and Monday 7 December at Newlands in Cape Town. CSA lists all three at 13:30. Check cricket.co.za for any change.
Where can I bet on Proteas vs Bangladesh in South Africa?
Any licensed South African bookmaker covers the series. Betway, Hollywoodbets, Sportingbet, 10bet and Supabets all have cricket markets. Compare the price on your market at two or three of them before you bet.
What happens to my bet if an ODI is shortened by rain?
Most bookmakers settle the match result on the official result, including Duckworth-Lewis-Stern results. Runs and player markets often need a minimum number of overs to stand. Rules differ by bookmaker, so read them before you bet.
Is the Proteas match winner market worth betting?
When the Proteas are short favourites at home, the match winner pays little. Many punters look at the series result, top batter, total runs or the innings runs line instead. Only bet a short price if you think it is still too long.
