Voucher Deposits for Betting in South Africa (2026): 1Voucher, OTT, Blu & Kazang

Published March 3, 2026 • 9 min read • Brandon K

Not everyone has a bank account. Not everyone wants to use one for betting. And in South Africa, you don't have to. Prepaid vouchers are one of the most popular ways to fund a betting account — walk into a shop, buy a voucher with cash, punch in the PIN, and you're playing within seconds.

It's the same concept as buying airtime, except the credit goes to your bookmaker account instead of your phone. No bank account needed, no card details, no waiting for EFT to clear. Just cash in, voucher code, bet.

This guide covers every voucher method available for betting in South Africa — 1Voucher, OTT Voucher, Blu Voucher, and Kazang. Which bookmakers accept them, where to buy them, and how to deposit step-by-step. If you prefer paying cash or don't have access to digital banking, this one's for you.

Key Takeaway: Voucher deposits are instant, require no bank account, and are accepted at most major SA bookmakers. 1Voucher has the widest retail availability (Pick n Pay, Shoprite, Checkers, SPAR). Hollywoodbets is the most voucher-friendly bookmaker in SA.

Why Use Vouchers for Betting?

Before we get into the specifics, here's why vouchers are worth considering — even if you do have a bank account:

✅ Voucher Pros

  • No bank account required — cash only
  • Instant deposits — funds available in seconds
  • No card details stored online
  • Available at thousands of retail outlets
  • Good for budgeting — buy only what you want to bet
  • No transaction fees at most bookmakers

❌ Voucher Cons

  • Need to physically visit a store
  • Can't deposit at 2am from your couch (stores closed)
  • Maximum voucher values are limited
  • Some voucher types have expiry dates
  • Not all bookmakers accept all voucher types
  • Withdrawals still need a bank account at most bookmakers

The big one is accessibility. Millions of South Africans don't have traditional bank accounts but still have access to retail stores. Vouchers bridge that gap completely. And even if you do bank with Capitec or FNB, using vouchers means your betting deposits never show on your bank statement — which some punters prefer.

The 4 Main Betting Voucher Types in SA

1Voucher

1Voucher is the big one. It's the most widely available prepaid voucher in South Africa, sold at major retailers across the country. If you can buy groceries, you can buy a 1Voucher.

  • Where to buy: Pick n Pay, Shoprite, Checkers, SPAR, Boxer, Game, Makro, and thousands of independent retailers
  • How it works: Ask the cashier for a 1Voucher, pay with cash, get a receipt with a unique PIN code
  • Denominations: Typically R10, R20, R50, R100, R200, R500, R1,000
  • Expiry: Usually 12 months from purchase — check your receipt
  • Fees: No fees from the retailer. No fees from the bookmaker. The full amount goes to your account.

1Voucher is the one we recommend for most punters. The retail network is massive — you're never far from a 1Voucher outlet, whether you're in Sandton or a small town in Limpopo.

OTT Voucher

OTT Voucher is another popular option, especially in Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal. It works similarly to 1Voucher but has a slightly different retail footprint.

  • Where to buy: Selected retailers, OTT Voucher agents, some spaza shops
  • Denominations: R10 to R3,000 (varies by retailer)
  • Expiry: 24 months typically
  • Bonus: Some bookmakers run OTT-specific deposit bonuses from time to time

OTT Voucher has slightly higher maximum denominations than 1Voucher at some outlets, which is useful if you want to deposit larger amounts without buying multiple vouchers.

Blu Voucher

Blu Voucher is growing in popularity and accepted at an increasing number of SA bookmakers. It's particularly strong in KwaZulu-Natal and Gauteng.

  • Where to buy: Retail stores with Blu Voucher terminals, selected spazas and convenience stores
  • Denominations: R10 to R1,000
  • Expiry: Check receipt — typically 12-24 months
  • Note: Fewer retail outlets than 1Voucher, but expanding

Kazang Vouchers

Kazang is different — it's a terminal-based system found primarily in informal retail (spazas, small shops, petrol station convenience stores). If you live in an area where the nearest Pick n Pay is a bus ride away but there's a spaza on the corner, Kazang might be your best option.

  • Where to buy: Kazang-enabled terminals at spazas, informal retailers, and convenience stores
  • Denominations: R10 to R500 (lower maximum than 1Voucher)
  • Availability: Over 100,000 Kazang terminals across SA — excellent in townships and rural areas
  • Expiry: Varies — always check

Kazang's strength is reach. In areas where major retailers are scarce, Kazang terminals fill the gap. The Kazang network was built for airtime and electricity, but betting vouchers have become a significant part of their offering.

How to Deposit with a Voucher: Step by Step

The process is nearly identical across all bookmakers and voucher types. Here's the general flow:

Step 1: Buy the Voucher

  1. Go to any participating retailer (Pick n Pay, Shoprite, SPAR, etc.)
  2. Ask the cashier for a voucher — specify the type (1Voucher, OTT, Blu, or Kazang) and the amount you want
  3. Pay with cash
  4. Keep the receipt — it has your unique voucher PIN printed on it
Important: Keep the receipt safe. The PIN on it is like cash — if you lose it, the money is gone. Some stores print the PIN separately; others include it on the main receipt. Double-check before leaving the till.

Step 2: Log Into Your Bookmaker Account

  1. Open your bookmaker's app or website
  2. Go to the Deposit section (usually a "+" icon or "Deposit" button)
  3. Select the voucher type that matches what you bought (e.g., "1Voucher" or "OTT Voucher")

Step 3: Enter the Voucher PIN

  1. Type in the PIN from your receipt — usually 12-16 digits
  2. Confirm the deposit amount
  3. Hit "Deposit" or "Submit"

Step 4: Bet

That's it. The funds appear in your account instantly — usually within 5-10 seconds. No waiting, no "pending" status, no verification delays. You're ready to bet immediately.

Voucher Tips and Common Mistakes

  1. Check the expiry date. Every voucher has one. 1Voucher is typically 12 months, OTT Voucher is 24 months, but always check the receipt. An expired voucher is worthless, and refunds are difficult to impossible.
  2. Keep the receipt. If something goes wrong — the deposit doesn't process, you enter the wrong PIN, or the system glitches — you'll need the receipt as proof of purchase. Take a photo of it as backup. Seriously.
  3. Enter the PIN carefully. One wrong digit and the deposit fails. If you've tried multiple times and it's still not working, contact the bookmaker's support before the voucher gets locked. Most systems lock a voucher after 3-5 failed PIN attempts.
  4. Buy from reputable retailers. Stick to major chains (Pick n Pay, Shoprite, SPAR, Checkers) for 1Voucher and OTT. For Kazang, use established spazas you trust. Voucher fraud exists — if someone offers you a voucher "at a discount," it's probably already been redeemed or is stolen.
  5. Vouchers are one-time use. If you buy a R200 voucher and deposit R200, the voucher is done. You can't split it across multiple deposits. If you want to deposit R100 now and R100 later, buy two R100 vouchers instead.
  6. Withdrawals are separate. Here's the catch — you can deposit via voucher, but you can't withdraw to a voucher. Most bookmakers require a bank account for withdrawals (EFT, Capitec Pay, etc.). If you don't have a bank account, check if your bookmaker offers alternative withdrawal methods. Hollywoodbets has branch cash withdrawals in some cases.
  7. Budget with vouchers. One of the best things about voucher deposits is forced budgeting. You can't impulsively deposit R5,000 from your phone at midnight — you have to physically go to a shop, buy the voucher, and make a conscious decision. This is actually a responsible gambling feature, even if it's not marketed that way.

Responsible Gambling with Vouchers

One genuinely good thing about voucher deposits: they naturally limit how much you can bet. You have to leave the house, go to a shop, and physically buy the voucher. That friction is healthy. It gives you time to think about whether you really want to deposit more.

Some tips for keeping it responsible:

  • Set a weekly voucher budget. Decide on Monday how much you'll spend this week — buy that amount in vouchers and nothing more.
  • Don't buy vouchers on impulse. If you've just lost and you're driving past a Shoprite thinking "just one more R200 voucher" — don't. Go home. Come back tomorrow with a clear head.
  • Use the deposit limits. Most bookmakers let you set daily, weekly, or monthly deposit limits on your account. Set one and stick to it.
