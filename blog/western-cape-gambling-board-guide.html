Western Cape Gambling Board: How to Check a Licence, Complain or Self-Exclude

Published 12 November 2026 · Sources checked 12 November 2026

Most of the big South African betting brands are licensed in one province: the Western Cape. The regulator behind those licences is the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB). If you bet online in South Africa, there is a good chance the bookmaker you use answers to this board, so it is worth knowing what it does, how to check a licence on its list, and where to go when something goes wrong.

This guide uses only what the board and the National Gambling Board publish themselves. We checked every list, email address and phone number below on 12 November 2026.

WCGRB at a glance
  • Full name: Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (Wes-Kaapse Raad op Dobbelary en Wedrenne · Ibhodi Yelentshona Kapa Yokungcakaza Ngemali Neyemidyarho)
  • Law it works under: the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Act, 1996
  • Website: wcgrb.co.za
  • Phone: +27 21 480 7400 · General email: info@wcgrb.co.za
  • Bookmaker complaints and disputes: Complaints.Compliance@wcgrb.co.za
  • Licensed bookmakers on its list: 68 companies (licence holders page, 12 Nov 2026)

What the Western Cape Gambling Board does

The board describes its main purpose as controlling and regulating gambling and racing in the Western Cape. In practice, that means four jobs:

  • Licensing. It receives, investigates and decides licence applications, including probity checks on the people behind a company.
  • Oversight. It sets licence conditions and runs compliance and financial audits on licence holders.
  • Enforcement. Its Law Enforcement Unit raids illegal operators. Its website reports 1,004 closures, 8,904 items seized and 8,120 items destroyed.
  • Responsible gambling. It runs self-exclusion and a problem-gambling self-assessment, and refers people to the South African Responsible Gambling Foundation.

The board's FAQ also lists what it does not license. It doesn't license bingo halls or electronic bingo terminals, apart from bingo run to raise funds. It doesn't license poker rooms either: poker outside a licensed casino is illegal in the province, and the board asks people to report poker rooms to its compliance manager on 021 480 7423.

Two things to notice. First, the licensed address isn't always in the Western Cape: a company can hold a Western Cape bookmaker licence and run its offices from KwaZulu-Natal or Gauteng. Second, the board's own list does change. If a brand you use isn't on it, check the register again before you assume the worst, then check which province the operator says licenses it. How to check a betting site's licence in 4 steps

  1. Find the licence line on the site. Licensed South African bookmakers state their licensing board, and usually a licence number, in the footer or the terms and conditions. Copy the company name and number exactly.
  2. Search the NGB register. Open the verified operators register and search for the brand or company name. Check that the licence number and province match what the site claims.
  3. Cross-check the board's own list. For a Western Cape licence, the company should also appear under "Bookmakers" on the WCGRB licence holders page.
  4. Treat gaps as a warning. If a site gives only an offshore licence, no licence at all, or a number nobody lists, don't deposit. The NGB says plainly: don't gamble anywhere that isn't on its list. Our licensed vs illegal betting sites guide covers the other red flags.
💡 Tip: Do this check before you send FICA documents. An unlicensed site that asks for your ID, proof of address and bank details is a bigger risk than one that only takes a deposit. Our FICA verification guide explains what a legitimate bookmaker will ask for.

A note on "online gambling is illegal"

The board's FAQ says that under the National Gambling Act (Act 7 of 2004), "any form of online gambling is illegal". That line confuses many punters, because the same board licenses dozens of bookmakers that take bets through websites and apps. The distinction is between online casino-style games, which the national law doesn't allow, and sports and horse-racing betting with a provincially licensed bookmaker, which is what those licences cover. If a "betting site" mainly pushes slots or live casino tables, that's the part the FAQ is warning about.

How to complain about a Western Cape-licensed bookmaker

The board takes complaints and disputes about casinos, LPMs, the totalisator and bookmakers it licenses. A complaint is easier to resolve if you go in organised:

  1. Raise it with the bookmaker first, in writing (email or the support chat), and keep the reference number.
  2. Collect evidence: your account username, the bet slip or transaction ID, dates and times, amounts, and screenshots of the bet and of any messages.
  3. Email the board at Complaints.Compliance@wcgrb.co.za. Name the operator (brand and company name from the register), say what happened, attach your evidence and say what outcome you want.
  4. Keep everything. Save the board's reply and any case number.

Common disputes are withdrawals held for FICA, voided bets and bonus terms. Before escalating a withdrawal problem, compare it with the timelines in our bookmaker withdrawal times guide, because some delays are normal verification rather than a breach.

Reporting illegal gambling

Information about unlawful gambling in the Western Cape, whether an unlicensed site, a backroom machine operation or a poker room, goes to Enforcement@wcgrb.co.za. Corruption within the industry is reported separately to the Public Service Corruption Hotline (integrity@publicservicecorruptionhotline.org.za). Nationally, the NGB takes reports on 010 003 3475 or info@ngb.org.za.

Self-exclusion and the self-assessment

If betting is costing more than you planned, the board gives you two routes:

  • Self-assessment: a short questionnaire on the WCGRB website.
  • Self-exclusion: email selfexclusions@wcgrb.co.za, or start a referral through the Responsible Gambling Foundation's digital referral, which the board links to.

You can also set deposit limits or take a break directly in your bookmaker account. Our responsible gambling page explains how.

Open board meetings

Section 18(7) of the provincial Act makes the board's meetings open to the public, and the board holds quarterly open meetings at its office and on Zoom. Its 2026 dates were 31 March, 30 June, 29 September and 24 November 2026. To attend, complete the registration form on the About page and email it to openmeetings@wcgrb.co.za.

Other provincial gambling boards

Each province has its own regulator. If your bookmaker is licensed elsewhere, complaints go to that board instead. Gauteng's is the Gauteng Gambling Board (011 581 4800). Frequently asked questions

What is the Western Cape Gambling Board?

It is the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Board (WCGRB), the provincial regulator that licenses and oversees bookmakers, casinos, limited payout machines, the horse-racing totalisator and gambling equipment suppliers in the Western Cape, under the Western Cape Gambling and Racing Act, 1996.

How do I check if a betting site is licensed in the Western Cape?

Find the licence number in the site's footer or terms, then search for it on the National Gambling Board's verified operators register and on the WCGRB licence holders page. The company name, licence number and province should all match.

How do I lodge a complaint with the Western Cape Gambling Board?

Raise the issue with the bookmaker in writing first. If it isn't resolved, email Complaints.Compliance@wcgrb.co.za with your account details, bet or transaction IDs, dates, amounts and screenshots.

What is the Western Cape Gambling Board's phone number?

The board's general number is +27 21 480 7400, and its general email is info@wcgrb.co.za. Poker room reports go to 021 480 7423.

Can I self-exclude through the Western Cape Gambling Board?

Yes. Email selfexclusions@wcgrb.co.za, or start a self-exclusion through the Responsible Gambling Foundation's digital referral at responsiblegambling.org.za.
