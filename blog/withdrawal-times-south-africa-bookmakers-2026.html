Withdrawal Methods Explained

South African bookmakers offer several withdrawal methods. Here's what each one means for your payout speed:

Bank EFT (Electronic Funds Transfer)

The most common withdrawal method. Money goes directly to your bank account — FNB, Capitec, Standard Bank, Nedbank, or Absa. It's reliable but slow.

  • Typical time: 1–3 business days (some bookmakers up to 5)
  • Processing: Bookmaker processes the request, then your bank takes 1–2 days
  • Weekend requests: Won't process until Monday
  • Best for: Large withdrawals, any bank account

FNB eWallet

The fastest way to get your winnings if you have FNB access. Money is sent to your cellphone number and you collect cash from any FNB ATM using a PIN.

  • Typical time: Under 24 hours (often within a few hours)
  • Daily limit: R3,000 per day at most bookmakers
  • How to collect: Dial *120*277# for your PIN, then visit any FNB ATM
  • Best for: Quick access to smaller amounts, no bank account needed

Instant Money (Nedbank / Standard Bank)

Similar to FNB eWallet but via Nedbank or Standard Bank's instant payment systems. Not as widely supported by bookmakers.

  • Typical time: Under 24 hours
  • Available at: Hollywoodbets, WSB, and a few others
  • Best for: Nedbank or Standard Bank customers who want fast access

Capitec Withdrawals

Capitec is South Africa's most popular bank, but most bookmakers only support Capitec via standard EFT. There's no "Capitec instant" option at most bookmakers.

  • Typical time: 1–2 business days via EFT (Capitec processes EFTs slightly faster than other banks in our experience)
  • Tip: Some punters report Capitec EFTs arriving within 24 hours from Hollywoodbets and Betway

What Slows Down Your Withdrawal?

Even with a fast bookmaker, several factors can delay your payout:

1. FICA Verification Not Complete

This is the number one reason for delayed first withdrawals. Every SA bookmaker requires FICA documents:

  • South African ID (or passport for non-citizens)
  • Proof of address — utility bill, bank statement, or municipal account (less than 3 months old)
  • Proof of bank account — bank statement or letter showing your name and account number

⚠️ Pro tip: Upload your FICA documents immediately after registering

Don't wait until your first withdrawal request. FICA verification can take 24–48 hours. Get it done early so there's no delay when you want to cash out.

2. Withdrawing More Than You Deposited

Some bookmakers have additional verification for large withdrawals (typically over R5,000 or R10,000). This can add 1–2 days to the process.

3. Weekend and Public Holiday Requests

Bank EFTs don't process on weekends or public holidays. A withdrawal requested on Friday afternoon won't start processing until Monday. FNB eWallet works 7 days a week, which is another reason to prefer it.

4. Pending Bet or Bonus Wagering

If you have active bets or haven't met bonus wagering requirements, your withdrawal may be delayed or partially blocked. Check your pending bets before withdrawing.

5. Bank Processing Delays

Even after the bookmaker releases your funds, your bank takes time to process the incoming EFT. In our experience:

  • Capitec: Usually fastest — often next business day
  • FNB: 1–2 business days
  • Standard Bank: 1–2 business days
  • Nedbank: 1–2 business days
  • Absa: 1–3 business days (occasionally slower)

Can Bookmakers Refuse to Pay Out?

This is a common worry for SA punters, especially those new to online betting. The short answer: licensed SA bookmakers cannot refuse a legitimate payout. They're regulated by provincial gambling boards, and withholding winnings would risk their licence.

However, there are legitimate reasons a payout might be blocked:

  • Incomplete FICA verification
  • Suspected bonus abuse or multi-accounting
  • Underage account (under 18)
  • Technical errors on the bookmaker's side (rare, but it happens)

If your withdrawal is refused and you believe it shouldn't be, contact the bookmaker's support team first. If that doesn't resolve it, you can lodge a complaint with the relevant provincial gambling board.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Betway withdrawal take in South Africa?

Betway EFT withdrawals take 1–3 business days. FNB eWallet is faster — usually under 24 hours. The minimum withdrawal is R10.

How long does a Hollywoodbets withdrawal take?

Hollywoodbets is the fastest SA bookmaker for withdrawals. FNB eWallet payouts typically arrive within 2–6 hours. Bank EFT usually takes 1 business day.

Can I withdraw to Capitec from betting sites?

Yes — all major SA bookmakers support Capitec withdrawals via bank EFT. There's no Capitec instant option, but EFTs to Capitec tend to process faster than other banks (often next business day).

What is FNB eWallet for betting withdrawals?

FNB eWallet sends your winnings to your cellphone number. You collect cash at any FNB ATM using a PIN (dial *120*277#). You don't need an FNB bank account. The daily limit is typically R3,000.

Why is my betting withdrawal taking so long?

The most common reasons: incomplete FICA verification, weekend/public holiday processing, pending bets or bonus wagering requirements, or large withdrawal amounts that need additional verification.

Do I have to pay tax on betting winnings in South Africa?

No — in South Africa, gambling winnings are not taxed as income for individual punters. The bookmaker pays a turnover tax, not you. Read our SA betting tax guide for full details.
