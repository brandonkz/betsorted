Two Local Bookmakers Worth Your Attention

When South African bettors talk about homegrown bookmakers, Hollywoodbets gets all the glory. But quietly operating in the background are two solid alternatives that regularly outperform on specific metrics: YesPlay and Gbets.

YesPlay launched in 2019 under the Grand Parade Investments umbrella — the same group behind GrandWest Casino in Cape Town. It's positioned as a premium, straightforward experience with clean odds and genuinely fast withdrawals. Gbets, run by the Goldrush Group out of Midrand, targets the value-conscious bettor with a no-deposit welcome bonus and arguably the best Lucky Numbers product in the country.

Both are fully FICA-compliant, licensed by provincial gambling boards, and have processed millions in withdrawals to South African bank accounts. So the question isn't which one is safe — they both are. The question is which fits your betting style in 2026.

Welcome Bonuses: Which Offer Is Actually Worth Taking?

This is where the two bookmakers split in philosophy.

YesPlay runs a classic 100% deposit match up to R1,000. Deposit R500, get R500 in bonus funds. The rollover requirement is 5x the bonus amount on odds of 1.40 or higher — that's among the more reasonable wagering requirements you'll find on SA betting sites. If you're planning to deposit R300–R1,000 anyway, this is real value.

Gbets takes the opposite approach: R50 free bet with no deposit required. Yes, that's R50 to bet with before you've put in a single rand. The catch? You need to wager the free bet amount 5x to unlock withdrawable winnings. On a R50 free bet, that's R250 in bets — very achievable across a weekend of PSL matches. After that, Gbets occasionally runs reload bonuses for existing customers and has a points-based loyalty system through the Goldrush Rewards programme.

Bottom line on bonuses: If you have R500+ to deposit, YesPlay's match bonus gives more raw value. If you want to test a bookmaker risk-free before committing, Gbets' no-deposit R50 free bet is the better entry point.

PSL Betting: Which Has Better Odds on South African Football?

We ran spot checks on PSL match odds across 12 fixtures during the 2025–26 season. Here's what we found:

YesPlay consistently offered competitive odds — usually within 2–3% of the market leader on a given match. They were strongest on Mamelodi Sundowns and Orlando Pirates matches (the big-draw games), where the liquidity is high. Their in-play betting interface is clean, and the cash-out feature on PSL accumulators works reliably.

Gbets was occasionally a fraction lower on match odds but made up for it with same-game multi options and slightly better prices on lesser-followed teams like Stellenbosch FC and Richards Bay. For PSL accumulators, Gbets' multi-bet interface is actually easier to use — you can build a parlay in under 30 seconds on the mobile app.

Neither bookmaker is going to blow you away with PSL odds the way a Pinnacle-style sharp book might. But both are solid for casual PSL betting, and both carry full markets: match result, both teams to score, correct score, first goalscorer, and corners.

Lucky Numbers: Where Gbets Dominates

If Lucky Numbers is your thing — and for millions of South Africans, it absolutely is — Gbets is in a different league.

Gbets offers multiple Lucky Numbers draws throughout the day, including their own proprietary draws alongside standard Lotto-based numbers. The variety of bet types (straight, combination, overall) and the ability to place R1 bets makes it accessible for anyone. The USSD betting option (*120*3652#) means you can place Lucky Numbers bets without data — critical for bettors in areas with limited connectivity.

YesPlay has Lucky Numbers, but the product is more basic. Fewer draws, fewer bet types, and no USSD support. If Lucky Numbers represents more than 20% of your betting activity, Gbets wins this category hands down.

Horse Racing

This one goes to YesPlay. As part of the Grand Parade Investments group — which has deep ties to SA racing through GrandWest — YesPlay's horse racing product is noticeably stronger. You get full SA race coverage (Turffontein, Kenilworth, Greyville) plus international racing from UK, Australia, and Hong Kong.

Gbets covers horse racing but it's clearly not their core product. The market depth on SA races is solid, but international racing is thin. If you're a serious racing punter who also wants to bet on football and cricket, YesPlay is the better one-stop shop.

Withdrawal Speed: Both Are Decent, Gbets Edges It

In our testing, Gbets was marginally faster on EFT withdrawals — typically 2–4 hours during business hours. YesPlay's EFT withdrawals took 2–6 hours on average, occasionally spilling into the next business day for late-afternoon requests.

Both bookmakers require FICA verification (ID + proof of address + proof of bank account) before your first withdrawal. Complete this upfront when you register so you don't hit delays when you actually want to cash out.

Both support standard SA payment methods:

  • EFT / bank transfer
  • Instant EFT (via Ozow or similar)
  • Debit card (Visa/Mastercard)
  • Capitec Pay (YesPlay specifically markets this)

Mobile App Experience

YesPlay has a polished Android and iOS app. The UI is clean, the in-play interface updates quickly, and the bet slip management is intuitive. It feels like a modern fintech product rather than an old-school betting site. Download size is around 45MB — reasonable for a betting app.

Gbets has an Android app and a mobile-optimised website. The app is functional but not flashy — it gets the job done. Where Gbets wins on mobile is their USSD system for feature phone users, which no other SA bookmaker does as well. If you're on a budget smartphone with limited storage, the lightweight Gbets mobile site is actually faster than a heavy app.

Responsible Gambling Tools

Both bookmakers offer deposit limits, cooling-off periods, and self-exclusion. YesPlay's responsible gambling section is more prominently displayed in-app (a good sign). Gbets also links clearly to the National Responsible Gambling Programme (0800 006 008).

Neither is a standout on this front compared to international operators, but both meet the minimum legal requirements for South African licensed bookmakers.

🏆 Overall Verdict: Gbets by a Nose

If you're a pure sports bettor who bets primarily on football and cricket, these two are essentially equal. The no-deposit free bet lowers the barrier to entry, the USSD system serves rural and township bettors that YesPlay completely ignores, and the Goldrush casino integration makes it a genuine all-in-one platform.

That said, if you're a horse racing fan or want the cleanest app experience in SA betting, YesPlay is genuinely excellent. The two bookmakers serve slightly different audiences, and the smart move is to have accounts with both — use each for what it does best.
